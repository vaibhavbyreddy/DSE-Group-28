8. Manufacturing, Assembly, and Integration Plan (MAI Plan):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item MAI Plan Overview: just mention that it goes from the first step manufacturing, then assembly, and finally integration. Also that there is a legend with the materials, manufacturing methods, and joining methods used.
    \item Manufacturing: split into two - manufacturing process for the carbon fibre composite, and then for the aluminium alloy. For the composite, different manufacturing techniques were considered based on labor, part size, and efficiency of manufacturing method (how time-consuming it is, material waste). The prepreg with autoclave came out on top. For the aluminium alloy, a research was conducted as to how the landing gear and pylons can be manufactured for our scale. Since the initial fleet is limited to 5 units, CNC machining is the preferred manufacturing technique, combined with deep hole drilling for the holes in the primary landing gear components. Then an overview of the heat treatment and corrosion protection processes was given.
    \item Assembly: different joining techniques established in the aerospace industry were listed: adhesive bonding, riveting, bolting, welding, along with their advantages and disadvantages. Adhesive bonding is selected for the composite parts, whereas the engine pylons and the landing gear component are going to be bolted. Also it was mentioned that specialized modular ports will join the primary subsystems (wing, fuselage, empennage).
    \item Integration: it goes over the integration of the remaining system like avionics, battery, engines, and interior within the assembled structure. The main considerations are accessibility and avoiding time delays and safety risk by impropersequencing.
\end{enumerate}

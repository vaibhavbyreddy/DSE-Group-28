2. Operations and Logistics
    Operations
    - Den Helder airport was chosen as the test take-off point based on NLR recommendations and the fact that the Netherlands was identified as the beachhead market. It is a joint civil and military airfield located close to the sea, and the airspace around it is controlled, which is good for the safety aspect and certification.
    - A test day timeline was drafted, highlighting the pre-, in-, and post-flight operations for each individual test, assuming 4 tests per day for 100 days a year. 
    - A preliminary maintenance and continuing plan was drafted based on EU No 1321/2014 and an analysis of stakeholder responsibilities.
    
    Logistics
    - HUGO is to be stored in a hangar located on Den Helder grounds.
    - Flights will be coordinated with De Kooy Air Traffic Control.
    - Den Helder is known for hosting off-shore flights above the North Sea.

4. Design Trade-Off
    - separate trade-offs were made for the configuration and materials, since our original configurations only vary in the types of ports and landing gear (not much to do with material selection)
    
    Trade-Off Criteria Selection
    - For configurations: versatility, reliability, cost, performance, sustainability.
    - For materials: structural properties, durability, material cost, manufacturing cost, design freedom, sustainability
    - no-go values were identified for each criterion
    
    Trade-Off Weight Factors
    - For configurations: analytic hierarchy process (AHP) was used to determine the weights.
    - For materials: weights were assigned arbitrarily based on an in-depth
    analysis of relative criterion importance. (wink wink)

    Material selection
    - HUGO was divided into several structural subsystems for which primary materials were chosen in a trade-off.
    - An analysis of reference flying testbeds (X56-and DAST ARW-2) and airliners (A350 XWB, B787) gave us an insight into industry trends and existing solutions.
    - In total, 7 candidate materials were identified. Their key structural properties (stiffness, buckling, strength, fracture toughness) were assessed and normalised w. r. t. density to generate a so-called total normalised economic factor.

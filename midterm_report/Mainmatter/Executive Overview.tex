To facilitate the operations and reduce transport costs, HUGO is to be stored in a hangar in the area adjacent to Den Helder airport. EASA Certification was also a major concern in the Baseline Report, as HUGO's risk class and operational environment were highly uncertain. However, in consultation with, and with guidance from EASA, HUGO was classified under CS-23 for aerobatic aircraft, with supplemental special conditions for unmanned aerial vehicles considered high risk. Successful certification of a UAV with such a high degree of modularity would require constant and expensive certification. However, through the simultaneous application for a type certificate and a Design Organisation Approval (DOA), HUGO will be allowed to undergo major changes without requiring full recertification. Though more versatile, it is a process that will require an initial investment of \euro{10170} for the certification, and then a yearly fee of \euro{11050} for the maintenance of the Type Certificate. For compliance, a team of engineers will therefore have to apply for the DOA, and be responsible for approving any major changes. A flight envelope combining both manoeuvrer and gust loads borne by the structure was then calculated to ensure compliance with the CS-23 aerobatic requirements. Furthermore, it was necessary to verify the present environmental requirements issued by the European Union. The relevant legalisation for UAVs of HUGO's specifications emphasise a focus on the reduction of noise and emissions.   

To determine if each of the five configurations complied with the requirements, a cursory analysis and estimation of design parameters had to be performed. Several trade-off criteria were established for parts of the design. First, the airfoil and analogous wing estimation was performed. To do that, performance and geometric parameters were obtained from reference aircraft and drag buildup methods described by Torenbeek, namely: stall speed, AR, wingspan, MAC, sweep, wing area, wing loading, and taper ratio. Then, design aerodynamic parameters were estimated using the previously obtained data and drag build-up methods described by Raymer (design lift coefficient, cruise Reynolds number, maximum lift coefficient). Lastly, a symmetrical supercritical airfoil (NASA SC(2)-0012) was selected to ensure successful operations at test conditions.

Once the final configuration was established, a Manufacturing, Assembly and Integration (MAI) plan was created. The manufacturing techniques were chosen based on labour costs, part size, production time, and material efficiency. The chosen materials, carbon fibre composite and Al 7075 T76, require different manufacturing processes. The carbon fibre parts will be manufactured using a pre-preg and autoclave method. Due to the primary use of the aluminium alloy for the landing gear, and the limited number of HUGOs set to be produced, CNC machining paired with deep-hole drilling, heat treatment, and corrosion protection was chosen as the manufacturing method. The assembly will involve the joining of different parts. As such, different joining techniques such as adhesive bonding, riveting, bolting, and welding were analysed, and the pros and cons of each method described for each planned application. Additionally, it was determined that the specialised modular ports will join the primary structure subsystems. The integration of the remaining systems (i. e. avionics, power supply, engine, and interior) were done in \autoref{sec:Integration}. The main considerations when planning the integration of subsystems were: accessibility of the components for maintenance, and avoiding time delays and safety risks due to improper subsystem integration.

The external configuration of the chosen design was illustrated through a sketch, elucidating the positions of all outside mounted ports and holes, such as antennas. The internal configuration is not yet defined at this design stage. Instead, all relevant components that must be contained within the fuselage are listed. A detailed explanation of the electronics system, major components, as well as specific components like the autopilot, and a comprehensive list of sensors used to quantify aeroservoelastic phenomena is provided further. It was determined that the battery will likely be the limiting factor dictating the dimensions of the fuselage, as it is the largest component. The fuel and propulsion system was also analysed and visualised graphically. The components related to the propulsion system were identified. Additionally, the components were sized to ensure compliance with the 5 litre payload requirement. The required positioning of the landing gear and the associated retraction mechanisms were also estimated and accounted for in the internal volume of the fuselage. Subsequently, a cost analysis of all components will be performed.

   
10. Sustainable development strategy.

To ensure minimal environmental impact, HUGO's sustainable development strategy was devised. A preliminary LCA analysis was performed, resulting in an estimated total Global Warming Potential (GWP) of $1.92794\cdot10^{6}$ [kg CO2 eq] for the production, operation and end-of-life solutions, with fuel-burn and the production of SAF being the biggest contributors. The social sustainability strategy from the Baseline Report was expanded on with the recommendations set out by the European Commission in regulation (EU) 2018/1139 Annex IX.

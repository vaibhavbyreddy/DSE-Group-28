For the purpose of this application, autopilot VECTOR-600 \footnote{\href{https://www.uavnavigation.com/products/autopilots/vector-600}{Vector-600}(accessed on 13.05)} was chosen as it incorporates many of the basic air sensors needed to conduct the flight. Additionally, this autopilot has been qualified by the industry, being tested against vibrations and crash loads. It incorporates: Dual IMU, redundant inertial sensing for altitude, magnetometer, gyroscope, inertial magnetometer, altimeter, airspeed sensor, GNSS receiver for gps signal. One limitation of this system is the limit of the air speed calculator, which can only reach speeds of 450 kts, while HUGO will fly at a maximum speed of 500 kts, as such an additional speed sensor will be needed. Alongside the autopilot, the design team has decided to include additional data sources such as: pitot static tube, radar system, temperature sensors, humidity sensors, cameras (one for piloting and 2 more for the view of the fuselage), and an agle of attack sensor.

\subsection{Fuel and propulsion system}
\label{subsec:fuel_systems}
Systems related to propulsion, including the turbojet engine, fuel tanks, fuel management and distribution systems are included in this category. The propulsion system is integrated into the fuselage group to allow for wing versatility, provide clean airflow over the wing and prevent aerodynamic interference of the engine with the wings. This has been determined for all of the configurations in \autoref{ch:trade-off}.\\

Based on the preliminary aircraft sizing and requirements, the initially selected VULCAN engine is in an early development stage, and the figures regarding its overall propulsive efficiency are uncertain. \todo{good to refer to the actual section where this is said} Furthermore, small jet engines currently on the market can be integrated easily due to their known characteristics and availability of the supporting subsystems they require to operate. Therefore, another turbojet engine candidates were considered. The industry-proven PBS TJ40-G2 engine is considered as a serious candidate for a HUGO engine and is used as a reference component in \autoref{}.\\

The turbojet engine needs several additional components to become operational. An ECU (Electronic Control Unit) is neccessary to correctly set the correct fuel mixture based on the thrust set and the fuel used. This unit is included inside the engine casing for the considered TJ40-G2 engine. Next, the fuel system consists of the fuel pump, fuel filter, shut-off valve and fuel distribution hoses. An overview of the propulsion system components is presented in \autoref{tab:prop_system} and a simple diagram of the subsystem relationships is shown in \autoref{fig:prop_diagram}. Since no available data about the TJ40-G2 fuel pump, fuel filter and power electronics board could be found, accessories from another manufacturer, JetCat, are used for reference. A slight margin has been added to the dimensions and weights of these components to generate a conservative estimate. The weight and dimensions of the power distribution system have been estimated based on a universal BMS \footnote{\href{https://www.orionbms.com/downloads/documents/orionjr2_specifications.pdf}{Lithium Ion Battery Management

\subsection{Payload systems}
\label{subsec:payload_systems}
\textbf{Internal Cargo}
Additionally, HUGO will have the capacity to allow for extra payload to be mounted onboard, such as sensors and equipment needed for research. As stated by the mission requirements, the payload must have a cargo volume of at least 5 litres and support an additional weight of 5 kg. The location of the cargo bay will be in front of the wings, close to the leading edge as to have a minimum impact on the dynamic stability characteristics of the aircraft.
This choice was made because the engine and fuel of the UAV will be placed behind the trailing edge of the plane, affecting the longitudinal trim balance of the aircraft.  

A more detailed analysis of the internal payload mounting and contents will be created in the following step of the design.

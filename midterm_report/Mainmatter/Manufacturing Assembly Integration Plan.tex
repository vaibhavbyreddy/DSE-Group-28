\subsection{Composites}
Considering that a carbon fiber composite will be used for the wings, fuselage, and empennage, the following manufacturing techniques are applicable \cite{Jabaar2021}, grouped by process category:

\textbf{Open mold processes}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textit{\textbf{Hand layup}}: most common composite manufacturing technique. Requires minimal equipment and low cost, but skilled labor. It is an open molding process --- a single-sided mold (positive or negative) is used for fabrication. It is especially suitable for the production of large shapes. It is inexpensive and requires little equipment, but depends heavily on skilled labor. First, the mold is cleaned and coated with a gel layer. Reinforcement material is then saturated with resin and applied layer by layer until the required thickness is reached. The laminate is compacted with brushes or rollers to eliminate air pockets and distribute the resin evenly. After curing, the component is removed from the mold, trimmed, and polished.

    \item \textit{\textbf{Vacuum resin infusion}}: Vacuum pressure is used to draw resin into the mold cavity in this two-sided molding process, which combines a single-sided mold with a vacuum bag. Fiber layers are first positioned in the mold, after which resin is sucked into the cavity. Atmospheric pressure helps compact the laminate and remove trapped air bubbles.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Closed mold processes}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
    \item \textit{\textbf{Resin transfer molding (RTM)}}: Unlike vacuum resin infusion, this process injects resin into the mold cavity under high positive pressure. The reinforcement layers are placed in the mold beforehand and enclosed with a rigid top mold. The thermosetting resin cures while under pressure, and the part is removed once sufficient crosslinking is achieved, followed by post-curing for complete curing. Components made with this method have smooth surface finishes and excellent mechanical properties, including high stiffness, strength, and impact resistance.

    \item \textit{\textbf{Compression molding}}: Compression molding is used to manufacture composite parts from dry chopped fibers mixed with resin. In this method, the laminate is placed between heated matched metal molds inside a compression press. Heat and pressure are applied to rapidly cure the material. The process requires minimal post-processing, reducing labor costs, and can be used for both thermosetting and thermoplastic composites. It is well suited for high-volume production of complex, high-strength parts with accurate dimensions, while built-in cooling systems help speed up the production cycle.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Prepreg-based processes}
\begin{enumerate}[resume]
    \item \textit{\textbf{Prepreg}}: Prepregs are pre-impregnated reinforcement plies used as the primary material for manufacturing high-performance composites in industries such as aerospace, automotive, sports, and ballistics. Considered one of the most advanced composite fabrication methods, prepreg processing involves layup, curing, and finishing. During curing, the material requires elevated temperature and pressure for proper consolidation, typically achieved through autoclave curing.

    \item \textit{\textbf{Autoclave}}: An autoclave is a pressure vessel used to create controlled temperature and pressure conditions, known as the curing cycle, for material processing. It is commonly used for composites as well as thermoplastics, metals, and ceramics. Although it produces high-quality parts, the process is energy-intensive and can generate considerable material waste.
\end{enumerate}

Based on the comparison of available methods and their respective advantages and limitations, \textbf{prepreg manufacturing with autoclave curing} emerges as the most suitable option for the fabrication of the wings, fuselage, and empennage. As large primary structures with compound curvature and stringent aerodynamic surface tolerances, these components demand a process that offers both geometric flexibility and precise control over fibre architecture. Prepreg layup satisfies both requirements: the ply-by-ply process accommodates complex geometries across large surface areas, while allowing full control over fibre orientation, which is essential for the aeroelastic tailoring of a HAR wing.

Unlike traditional wet layup processes, prepregs eliminate the need for manual resin application, ensuring consistent fibre-to-resin ratios throughout the entire composite structure, which translates to superior mechanical properties, excellent surface finish, and predictable performance in the final product.

Prepreg layup requires skilled labor, but significantly less so than wet hand layup, where resin mixing and application introduce operator-dependent variability. The material arrives ready to use, with resin already uniformly distributed, reducing the skill burden on resin management while concentrating it on ply placement and orientation --- a more controllable and trainable task. Additionally, it is faster than vacuum resin infusion because it eliminates the infusion step.

With a production run of only 5--10 units, high-volume techniques such as compression molding and RTM are difficult to justify. Compression molding is attractive for cutting cycle times, but this advantage is irrelevant at the present scale. Prepreg with autoclave cure, by contrast, delivers significant process efficiencies even at low volumes: it reduces labour costs by up to 40\% through less hands-on processing time, cuts rework by 60\% due to fewer defects from the outset, and achieves 30--50\% faster cycle times by eliminating the waiting associated with resin infusion \footnote{\href{https://incomepultrusion.com/pre-preg-comprehensive-guide-on-definition-characteristics-types-and-applications/}{The Complete Engineer's Guide to Prepreg Composites: What You Need to Know}}. \newcounter{fnprepreg} \setcounter{fnprepreg {\value{footnote}}
 
The desired performance imposes the most demanding structural requirements of any of the criteria above. Prepreg achieves void content below 0.5\% for premium grades, thickness tolerances of $\pm 0.05$\,\si{mm}, and surface finish Ra $\leq 0.8$\,\si{\micro\metre} \footnotemark[\value{fnprepreg}] - all of which are necessary for the structural baseline of a research airframe whose aeroelastic behaviour must be predictable and repeatable across test campaigns.

\subsection{Metal Alloys}
An aluminium alloy was selected as the manufacturing material for components such as landing gear struts, engine pylons, and related parts. The sequence of manufacturing steps is listed below.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Forging}: It is the preferred process for the manufacturing of the primary landing gear components \footnote{\href{https://www.aero-mag.com/why-additive-is-coming-into-landing-gear}{Why additive is coming into landing gear}}. Heated high-strength alloys are plastically deformed through the application of compressive forces using dies or plates to achieve the desired shape. In general, forging can be classified into two main categories \cite{}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{\textit{Open die forging}}: The workpiece is compressed by two parallel plates. The material is free to deform at the open edges.
        \item \textbf{\textit{Closed die forging}}: The workpiece is compressed between two parallel dies, allowing the material to deform freely at the exposed surfaces.
    \end{itemize}
    Common uses for closed die forging in the aerospace industry include manufacturing the landing gear axles, struts, and hinge fittings, as well as the engine mounts \footnote{\href{https://www.anchorharvey.com/knowledge-center/open-vs-closed-die-forgings-in-aerospace-applications/}{Open vs. Closed Die Forgings in Aerospace Applications}}. Some of the advantages of open-die forging are its relatively low tooling costs, making it well-suited for low- to medium-volume production, and the excellent control over grain flow, which enhances fatigue resistance and strength in critical applications. In contrast, closed-die forging is better suited for complex geometries requiring tight tolerances and high precision, and it generally leads to lower material waste \footnote{\href{https://mechforged.com/open-die-vs-closed-die-forging-which-to-use/}{Open Die vs Closed Die Forging: Which to Use?}}.  

    \begin{comment}
    \item \textbf{Investment Casting}: Investment casting, also known as lost-wax casting, involves creating a wax model of the desired component and coating it with a ceramic shell. The wax is then removed through heating, leaving a cavity into which molten metal is poured and allowed to solidify, forming the final part \cite{Lorraine2024}. High-strength alloys are commonly produced through investment casting for components such as brackets, links, fittings, and actuator bodies within landing gear systems. This process enables the production of near-net-shape components with tight dimensional tolerances, high-quality surface finishes, and complex geometries. In aerospace applications, these advantages contribute to reduced component weight, lower part counts, shorter manufacturing lead times, and a reduction in overall lifecycle costs \footnote{\href{https://www.epcast.com/aircraft-aerospace}{Aerospace Investment Casting}}.
    \end{comment}

    \item \textbf{CNC Machining}: All surfaces of the forged aluminium components require extensive CNC machining, with a substantial amount of material removal necessary for the inner cavity regions \footnote{\href{https://www.szsunbright.com/news-show-1033791.html}{The main manufacturing technology of landing gear}}.
    \newcounter{fnLG}
    \setcounter{fnLG}{\value{footnote}} Machine tools such as lathes, mills, and routers are controlled by pre-programmed software to cut, shape, and produce parts with high precision. Its main advantages include high accuracy and repeatability, improved production efficiency, reduced human error and material waste, and the ability to manufacture complex geometries consistently and safely \footnote{\href{https://www.goodwin.edu/enews/what-is-cnc/}{What is CNC Machining in Manufacturing?}}.

    \item \textbf{Deep hole drilling}: Many landing gear components (such as piston rods, outer cylinders, and axles) are long, slender parts made from difficult-to-cut ultra-high-strength titanium alloy. Conventional turning methods often fail to meet requirements due to severe tool wear, limited tool rigidity, and poor durability, making it difficult to achieve the required dimensional accuracy and surface quality, particularly in transition and fillet regions \footnotemark[\value{fnLG}]. Thus, dedicated deep hole drilling machines are required to meet the strict tolerances and demanding requirements of landing gear manufacturing, while enabling the machining of complex aerospace components \footnote{\href{https://www.aero-mag.com/unisig-b700-landing-gear-drilling-alta-precision}{Landing gear machining struts its stuff! }}.


    \item \textbf{Heat treatment}: Landing gears must perform under extreme loading conditions, imposing stringent performance requirements. Heat treatment can relieve internal stresses, enhance the strength, and increase the hardness of the aluminium alloy. In general, there are four types of heat treatment for aluminium alloys \footnote{\href{https://alloyheat.co.uk/4-types-of-heat-treatment-for-aluminium-alloys/}{4 Types Of Heat Treatment For Aluminium Alloys}}:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{\textit{Annealing}}: Resets the crystalline structure, relieving internal stresses and making the material easier to shape while increasing its resistance to deformation.

        \item \textbf{\textit{Solution Heat Treatment}}: Involves quenching the alloy, typically in water, to preserve the microstructure by preventing atomic redistribution. This prepares the aluminium for subsequent age hardening and enables additional forming operations.

        \item \textbf{\textit{Natural and Artificial Aging}}: Ageing enhances the strength and hardness of a solution heat-treated alloy. During this process, precipitates form from the solid solution elements, stabilising the alloy’s microstructure.

        \item \textbf{\textit{Homogenising}}: Aims to uniformly distribute alloying elements throughout the material to achieve consistent chemical composition, particularly in castings that solidify at varying rates.
    \end{itemize}
    

    \item \textbf{Surface Treatment and Protection}: To enhance the surface properties of aluminium products, including wear resistance, corrosion resistance, and reflectivity, a range of surface treatments has been developed. These processes are generally classified into three categories: electrochemical treatments, chemical treatments, and coatings \footnote{\href{https://www.totalmateria.com/en-us/articles/surface-treatment-of-aluminum/}{Surface Treatment Of Aluminum And Aluminum Alloys}}.
    
\end{enumerate}

Due to the large size and high strength requirements of the primary landing gear components, the forging process is divided into two sequential stages. The components first undergo open die forging to work the billet into a rough preform, refining the grain structure and achieving the approximate overall shape. They subsequently go through closed die forging to acquire the final near-net shape with the required dimensional precision and optimised grain flow alignment along the load paths. The combination of both methods leads to an optimisation of cost, material waste, and final mechanical properties of the forged aluminium alloy.

For the subsequent removal of excess material, CNC machining is selected for both the initial rough machining stage, during which the bulk of the material is removed, and the final precision machining stage, which achieves the tight tolerances and surface finish requirements of the component. CNC machining is well suited to this role due to its versatility — combining turning, milling, drilling, and grinding operations under pre-programmed software control — which minimises human error and enables the consistent production of the complex geometries present in landing gear components.

For the largest primary components such as the outer cylinder and piston rod, dedicated deep hole drilling machines are required to produce the central longitudinal bore. These are long, slender cavities with large depth-to-diameter ratios that cannot be achieved on conventional machining centres due to limitations in tool rigidity, chip evacuation, and coolant delivery at depth. Dedicated deep hole drilling machines address these constraints through specialised tooling and high-pressure coolant systems, enabling the required dimensional accuracy and surface finish to be achieved within the bore.

Following machining, the aluminium alloy components undergo heat treatment to achieve the required mechanical properties. Surface treatment follows as the final step, providing corrosion and wear protection by building a hard, corrosion-resistant oxide layer on the surface, followed by primer and topcoat application for additional environmental protection.

\subsection{Adhesive bonding}
Bonding is a joining method that uses an adhesive to connect two components. The adhesive transfers loads between the parts, ideally through shear. Tensile loading of the joint creates high stress concentrations, which can lead to premature failure.

Some advantages of this joining method include preserving the original material strength by avoiding holes and stress concentrations, maintaining a smooth surface without protruding fasteners, enabling the joining of dissimilar materials, and providing excellent resistance to corrosion. It also allows for air- and watertight sealing. There are also several disadvantages to bonding, including the need for surface pretreatment, the inability to disassemble joints, and the fact that it primarily carries transverse loads. Additionally, the manufacturing process can be relatively complex.

\subsection{Riveting}

Riveting is a widely used joining technique in the aerospace industry, where loads are transferred through fasteners or loaded pins. It is mainly used for joining thin sheet materials, typically only a few millimeters thick. However, rivets are generally unsuitable for composite materials because of bearing stress concerns; in such cases, bolts or alternative fastening methods are preferred.

Riveting offers several advantages, including high reliability, simple tooling, and straightforward inspection and repair processes. However, it also has drawbacks: rivets are susceptible to fatigue and corrosion, sensitive to leakage since they are not watertight, and may disrupt smooth aerodynamic surfaces. Additionally, they require holes that reduce material strength and are primarily designed to carry shear loads only.

\subsection{Bolting}
Bolted joints are similar to riveted joints in that they require comparable preparation steps, such as drilling and deburring, and they operate on similar load transfer principles. However, there are also several key differences: bolts can carry tensile loads, allow for disassembly, and are suitable for thicker sections. On the downside, they are generally more expensive than rivets.

\subsection{Welding}
A welding process joins two pieces of similar material to one continuous section. In case of metals
welding involves the local melting of material. Welding and riveting (sometime also bolting) are the most
interchangeable alternatives.

Welding offers several advantages, including a continuous load path, no need for overlapping components, and a resulting smooth surface. It also provides air- and watertight joints. However, it has notable drawbacks: the local melting of material alters the microstructure, which can lead to variations in quality along the weld length. For this reason, high-strength materials that rely on heat treatment to achieve their properties are generally unsuitable for welding. This is one of the key reasons why welding is rarely preferred over riveting in the aircraft industry.

\subsection{Selected assembly methods}

Thermoset carbon fibre composites are typically joined using adhesive bonding, as this is the most common practice in the aerospace industry. This method avoids drilling holes for fasteners, which would cut the continuous fibres and significantly weaken the structure. The joints between the primary subsystems (wing-to-fuselage) are hybrid: adhesive bonding combined with bolting, where the adhesive carries the distributed load and the bolts provide fail-safe redundancy and meet certification requirements.

The landing gear sub-components are assembled using bolted and lockbolted joints for rigid structural connections, and pin joints wherever relative motion is required, such as at the torque link and retraction actuator attachment points. Welding is excluded as a joining method for this application, as the local melting of material alters the microstructure and degrades the mechanical properties of the high-strength aluminium alloy.

Engine pylons are bolted to the fuselage structure. They carry very large, dynamic loads and must be removable for engine replacement and maintenance. Adhesive bonding alone cannot meet these requirements.

\section{Operations}
\label{sec:operations}
This Section contains a detailed description of the intended operations for HUGO, including an overview of the system users, testing location and conditions, mission phases, an approximate timeline. A brief discussion is additionally dedicated to operational constraints and maintenance procedures to ensure successful continuous operation of the UAV.

\subsection{High-level Mission Description}
The main objective HUGO is to simulate high subsonic flight of commercial aircraft, while simultaneously implementing AFS and GLA and recording relevant flight parameters. A mission is considered as successful if all the following apply:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The speed of Mach 0.75 was achieved and maintained for 5 consecutive minutes of straight flight.
    \item All control systems required for the specific test were activated and performed their duties without error.
    \item The flight data was successfully recorded, downloaded and provided to the customer.
\end{enumerate}

Market analysis and segmentation performed in the previous design phases highlighted four main sectors in which potential customers can be identified: universities and academic laboratories, research centres, aerospace companies, and defence\footnote{\label{url:baseline_report}\href{https://github.com/Teo-Cokonaj/DSE-28/blob/main/docs/Projects/DSE_Group_28_Baseline_Report.pdf}{HUGO Baseline Report} (accessed on 08.05.2026)}. Moreover, the Netherlands was identified as the beachhead market. Therefore, a suitable aerodrome was found in the north-east part of the country, Den Helder\footnote{\href{https://denhelderairport.nl/eng/}{Den Helder Aerport} (accessed on 22.05.2026)} serving as the test site take-off point. 

Several characteristics make Den Helder Airport the most suitable choice as the base of operation.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Firstly, it is located close to the North sea, only 5 km away, as such, HUGO will not have to spend a lot of time over inhabited land and will make it much faster to the testing site, over the Noth Sea. Its location also limits the certification requirements, which grow exponentially with density. 
    \item It is not a purely commercial airport that has regular flights. It is  a joint civil and military airfield, as such it is not congested and permits special operation missions such as the one for HUGO.
    \item It has a long paved runway, 1.27 km, that has emergency services available for the entire day, perfect for the operation of a test platform.
    \item It is operated in a controlled space, a requirement imposed by EASA regarding the certification \autoref{EASAInitialAirworthiness2025}.
\end{itemize}

At Den Hellen Airfield, HUGO will take off from runway 21 and depart via FOXTROT according to ATC instructions \footnote{\href{chrome-extension://efaidnbmnnnibpcajpcglclefindmkaj/https://opennav.com/pdf/EHKD/EH-AD-2.EHKD-VAC.pdf}{Den Helden departure map} (accessed on 11.05.2026)}. It will follow the departure regulations until it reaches a distance of 6 km from the airfield over the North Sea. Once it is clear of grease, it will start its altitude increase and route towards the testing area. The test will take place over the North Sea, and HUGO will follow the preplanned path, submitted in advance to the regional ATC.

\subsection{Mission Phase and Timeline}
A reference test day for HUGO is assumed to include four
test flights, with 100 operational days per year\footref{url:baseline_report}. Four main phases were identified for a test day: transport to test site, followed by pre-flight, in-flight, and post-flight operations. These shall now be evaluated in more detail.


\subsubsection{Transport to Test Site}
The day before a test flight is scheduled, the Terminal Aerodrome Forecast (TAF) shall be consulted to ensure that departure will be possible. In case of adverse weather prediction, the test day (or part of it) shall be cancelled and the customer shall be informed. 

In case the weather prediction allows for a successful take-off, the test day shall proceed according to schedule. If the test requires parts provided by the client to be mounted onto HUGO, these shall be integrated in the hangar, before proceeding onto the airfield. The ground station and fuel required for the flight shall also be transported onto the airfield.

\subsubsection{Pre-Flight Operations}
This subsection defines the pre-flight operations required before HUGO can proceed to the take-off checklist. The pre-flight phase begins once HUGO is unloaded from the transportation unit and ends when the aircraft is powered on, all checks are performed, and fuel is loaded on board. The objective of this phase is to verify that the mission plan, operating environment, ground support equipment, aircraft configuration, and safety provisions are suitable for the intended mission.

An overview of the pre-flight steps is provided in \autoref{fig:pre-flight_checklist}. Once HUGO is unloaded, the mission plan is reviewed, and NOTAMs are checked for restrictions and updates. If a go or conditional go is given, HUGO can be configured for the mission profile, and the crew briefing and preparation checklists can be done. Otherwise, the test shall be cancelled or delayed. Once all preparation steps are checked and recorded, HUGO can be considered as ready to fly, and the test personnel can proceed to the in-flight phase of the mission.

\subsubsection{Post-Flight Operations}
Once HUGO has performed a successful landing, the post-flight data extraction and operations can commence. In this mission phase, there are two possible scenarios,  depending on the mission profile of the day: HUGO can undergo preparations for the next test flight or undergo disassembly and reclaim procedures. An overview of operations for both scenarios are shown in \autoref{fig:post-flight_ops}.

If HUGO is set to go for another test flight, the obtained data shall be downloaded, and checks shall be performed for the internal and external systems to ensure operational readiness and safety. Next, the fuel tanks shall be refilled, enabling the mission to return to the in-flight phase.

Once HUGO has finished all tests scheduled for a specific flight day, the data from the last test shall be downloaded, and onboard system checks shall be performed. If no errors are detected, the power supply will be turned off, and an inspection of the external structure shall be performed. Next, the official debrief shall take place, and HUGO shall be disassembled and transported back to the hangar.

\subsection{Maintenance}
\label{subsec:maintenance}
To ensure the a safe and optimal operation of HUGO, regular maintenance shall be provided, along with regular repairs in case irregularities are found within a work day. Based on considerations made in previous design phases\footref{url:baseline_report}, a single HUGO is expected to undergo maintenance for 20\% of the working days, which comes down to 50 (working) days within a year when accounting for free days. Continuing airworthiness is the responsibility of the operator or, in case the operator is not able to comply with the regulations and standards imposed, an outside certified provider\footnote{\label{Easa_Maintenance}\href{https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/easy-access-rules/online-publications/easy-access-rules-continuing-airworthiness?erules-id=ERULES-1963177438-693}{Easy Access Rules for Continuing Airworthiness} (accessed on 11.05.2026)}.

\subsubsection*{General Overview}
At this stage of the design, the HUGO fleet is expected to feature five models in the initial development stage, allowing for the possibility of expansion. Having such a fleet size requires a dedicated location where maintenance can be provided for all working days. This problem is ultimately discussed in \autoref{sec:logistics}, where the storage of models is expanded upon.

The maintenance operations include: planning of operation, assessment of process, execution of repair/verification, inspection of aircraft ensure airworthiness, updating the records and release of the model. It is worth stating that modifications brought to the structure for the purpose of fitting a new configuration to be tested will be counted as maintenance. \subsubsection*{Continuing Airworthiness}
As stated previously, it is the operator's responsibility to ensure continuing airworthiness of an aircraft. For operators based in the EU, this typically entails appointing an approved Continuing Airworthiness Management Organisation (CAMO) \cite{EASA_CAirworthiness}. The responsibilities of CAMO include: development and control of the maintenance program, monitoring reliability and tracking compliance with Airworthiness Directives (ADs), as well as maintaining records and coordinating with authorities.

The steps towards establishing a cycle for maintaining airworthiness typically begins before the UAV is certified for operations. The competent national authority for operations based in the Netherlands is the Inspectie Leefomgeving en Transport (ILT)\footnote{\label{url:ILT}\href{https://www.ilent.nl/ilt-luchtvaartautoriteit}{Inspectie Leefomgeving en Transport - Over de ILT-Luchtvaartautoriteit} (accessed on 11.05.2026)}. The steps towards developing a maintenance cycle include developing safety and system design assurance, and then defining a continuing airworthiness concept. At this stage, the first draft of the Aircraft Maintenance Programme (AMP) is developed \cite{EASA_CAirworthiness}. The AMP shall be approved by the appointed CAMO organisation and renewed annually \footnote{\href{https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/the-agency/faqs/amp-aircraft-maintenance-programme}{EASA - Aircraft Maintenance Programme (AMP)} (accessed on 11.05.2026)}.

The AMP is part of Instructions for Continuing Airworthiness (ICA), which define the scope of limitations and maintenance task required to demonstrate that a design is compliant with certification requirements\footnote{\href{https://www.leanairline.com/blog/instructions-for-continuing-airworthiness}{Instructions for Continuing Airworthiness (ICA): From Certification to Operation} (accessed on 11.05.2026)}. ICA cover both scheduled and unscheduled maintenance procedures, and are necessary for obtaining a Type Certificate (TC). The relevance of a TC shall be explained in \autoref{ch:certification}.

Once the UAV is certified, CAMO takes the responsibility for scheduled maintenance enforcement, AD compliance, reliability tracking and Airworthiness Review Certificates \cite{EASA_CAirworthiness}. In case maintenance or repair procedures are required, a Maintenance, Repair, and Overhaul Organization (MRO) approved under EASA Part-145 shall be contracted to perform inspections, repairs, and modifications\footnote{\href{https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/acceptable-means-compliance-and-guidance-material-group/part-145-maintenance-organisation-approvals}{EASA Part-145 - Maintenance organisation approvals} (accessed on 11.05.2026)}. The MRO is additionally responsible for issuing certificates of release to service.

\section{Logistics}
\label{sec:logistics}
Logistics represent the backbone of each project, operation, and mission, encompassing the entirety of the unseen processes and actions that bring together the required resources to result in a functional product. For the proper operation of HUGO, a few logistical aspects have to be addressed before it is ready to perform operations. This Section addresses the key factors influencing the logistics of operations: required ground crew, storage facility, airfield and ground support, and the part supply chain.

 The majority of components will be ordered from certified providers, but large parts, such as the fuselage and wings, will be produced internally at a separate facility and subsequently transported to the airfield.

\subsection{Ground Crew Required for Operation}
\label{subsec:ground_crew}
A ground crew is required to maintain the readiness of HUGO and to set up the ground stations for monitoring. 

According to ICAO\cite{ICAO2011UAS}, a remote crew comprises licensed crew members whose duties are crucial for the in-flight operations of a remotely-piloted
aircraft. This includes the remote pilot in charge of manipulating flight controls, and visual observers (VO) who assist the operations by visually observing the UAV while it remains within line-of-sight. It is also expected that an ATC controller and personnel responsible for coordinating runway manoeuvres will have to be involved. Since there are no hard requirements regarding the minimum composition of the crew, at this stage of the design it is assumed that HUGO will require one pilot, one VO, and additional personnel as deemed appropriate by the airport authorities.

\subsection{Storage Facility}
\label{subsec:hangar}
On the ground, the aircraft will require an enclosed location to be stored in for maintenance and preparations. As mentioned in \autoref{subsec:maintenance}, the initial fleet is projected to comprise five HUGOs. This necessitates the use of a large hangar to house parts and machines needed for repairs. 

Den Helder airfield has hangar capabilities to house Project HUGO, although the conditions for using the facilities are not stated on their site. Multiple outside operators use the location as a primary air base of operations \footnote{\href{https://denhelderairport.nl/eng/airport/#faciliteiten}{Den Helder Facilities} (accessed on 12.05.2026)}. The availability of the hangar is based on request \footnote{\href{https://eaip.lvnl.nl/web/eaip/AIRAC\%20AMDT\%20012026\_2026\_01\_22/eAIP/EH-AD\%202\%20EHKD\%201-en-GB.html}{EHKD - DEN HELDER/DE KOOY} (accessed on 12 May 2026)}. The hangar is 51 m long, 31 m wide, and 11 m high, having a footprint of 151 $m^2$ \autoref{DenHelderAirport2013DHANieuwsbrief}. One model will have a wingspan of at most 15 m, and the space in the hangar is sufficient to fit up to 3 planes in line.  

Once the hangar is secured, it will function as the base of operation for the storage, maintenance and verification of HUGO before and after each flight. 

\subsection{Airfield and Ground Support}
\label{subsec:ground_support}
Additional logistical constraints stem from the use of an airfield, which introduces airfield operating costs, ATC coordination requirements, and the mapping of ground routes, taxi areas, and recovery zones. First, each drone flight must be approved via an application to De Kooy Air Traffic Control\footnote{\href{https://denhelderairport.nl/eng/flight-information/}{Flight information Den Helder Airport} (accessed on 12.05.2026)}. The departure and landing windows for HUGO will be influenced by the adjacent traffic, which necessitates accounting for multiple missed approaches or holding in the fuel budget. Additionally, Den Helder Airport charges a fee of \EUR{23.00} per landing for a fixed wing aircraft with MTOW below 1500kg\footnote{\href{https://denhelderairport.nl/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-fixed-wing-miv-01-01-2026-2.pdf}{Landing fees for fixed wing aircraft at Den Helder} (accessed on 12.05.2026)}.

Den Helder is known for hosting off-shore flights above the North Sea\footnote{\href{https://denhelderairport.nl/eng/columns/beveiliging-van-de-noordzee-begint-op-den-helder-airport/}{Security of the North Sea starts at Den Helder Airport!} (accessed on 12.05.2026)}, which matches the mission profile of HUGO described in \autoref{sec:operations}. Fuel availability is limited to JET A1\footnote{\href{https://denhelderairport.nl/eng/information-for-pilots/}{Information for pilots} (accessed 12.05.2026)}; using alternative fuels such as SAF would require individual approval from the airport authorities. 

Lastly, the environmental policy of Den Helder is aligned with the TU Delft drive towards inventing a sustainable future, which makes the partnership between the airport and HUGOs design team remarkably valuable. Over the past years, Den Helder has implemented LED lighting in its hangars, electric GPUs, baggage carts and tow trucks, as well as introduced a heat recovery installation system\footnote{\href{https://denhelderairport.nl/eng/duurzaamheid-op-den-helder-airport-dha/}{Sustainability at Den Helder Airport (DHA)} (accessed on 12.05.2026)}. Future plans include equipping all buildings with solar panels, heat pumps or heat recovery systems.

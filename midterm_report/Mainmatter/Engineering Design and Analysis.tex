\subsection{Analysis of Reference Aircraft}
The initial step in selecting an airfoil is to determine the design lift coefficient $C_{L_\text{des}}$ and maximum lift coefficient $C_{L_\text{max}}$. Due to the specified testing conditions, $C_{L_\text{des}}$ was selected for the Mach 0.75 airspeed condition at FL270 (8230 m). For initial estimates, the NASA DAST project is used as a reference\footnote{\href{https://web.archive.org/web/20230310084826/https://www.nasa.gov/centers/dryden/multimedia/imagegallery/DAST/DAST_proj_desc.html}{NASA - Firebee Drone: DAST} (accessed 07.05.2026)}. DAST was initially developed as a modified version of the Teledyne-Ryan BQM-34E/F Firebee II, with the purpose of remotely testing new aerodynamic designs in the 1980s. The aircraft was convenient for such testing due to easily replaceable wings and use of control surfaces mounted exclusively on the tail. The UAS was retrofitted with a supercritical wing aimed at achieving a flutter speed of Mach 0.95 at FL250, and used a custom control system for flutter suppression. 

To this day, the DAST aircraft is the only unmanned aircraft that approaches the proposed flight condition of HUGO in terms of flight speed and mission, and therefore considered as an appropriate reference point for the preliminary wing sizing.

\subsection{Cruise Reynolds number}
Based on the initial estimates from \autoref{tab:DAST_HUGO}, the Reynolds number for cruise $Re_{cr}$ and stall condition $Re_\text{stall}$ was calculated using \autoref{eq:Re}.
\begin{equation}
    Re = \frac{\rho cV}{\mu}
    \label{eq:Re}
\end{equation}
Using the cruise conditions $\rho_{cr}$, $V_{cr}$ and dynamic viscosity $\mu_{cr}$ corresponding to Torenbeek's ISA model \cite{AdvancedACDesign_Torenbeek}, a cruise Reynold's number $Re_{cr}$ of $6.2\cdot10^5$ was obtained for test conditions at FL270. \subsection{Supercritical airfoil}
Since the aircraft is expected to operate at Mach 0.75, it is necessary to take transonic phenomena into account. When flying in this region with a traditional airfoil, the airflow might locally be supersonic, which creates strong shock waves on the wing \cite{Whitcomb1974}. This leads to a large increase in drag, which is detrimental for the performance of the aircraft and introduces boundary layer separation. Such effects can be mitigated by the use of a supercritical airfoil. 

A supercritical airfoil is capable to operate at transonic conditions with only the onset of weak shock waves \cite{Harris1990}. An example of such airfoils used in the preliminary design for HUGO is a supercritical SC(2)-XXYY family developed by NASA, where XX is the airfoil design lift coefficient, and YY is the maximum thickness in percent \cite{Harris1990}. The advantage of using this system is the inclusion of the design lift coefficient, which makes it possible to narrow down the choices for HUGO using $C_{L_\text{des}}$ values found in \autoref{subsec:cl_des}.

As stated previously, the use of a supercritical wing profile is highly recommended for the considered test conditions. \subsection{Stall speed}
The next step is to identify the preliminary stall speed of HUGO. NASA does not provide the stall speed for the ARW-2 wing, however, it can be estimated based on the NASA SC(2) family of supercritical airfoils. The SC(2)-0412 is taken as a reference here. With a maximum lift coefficient $C_{L_{max}} \approx 1.55$ and $S = 0.1635 \ m^2$ at a mass $m = 50 \ kg$, the stall speed can be calculated using \autoref{eq:Vstall}.
\begin{equation}
    V_\text{stall} = \sqrt{\frac{2W}{\rho S C_{L_\text{max}}}}
    \label{eq:Vstall}
\end{equation}
From \autoref{eq:Vstall}, a stall speed $V_{stall} \approx 56 \ m \cdot s^{-1}$. A stall speed this large is highly likely to cause problematic landings. Therefore, adding high-lift devices to the aircraft or increasing wing area is inevitable. First, high-lift device addition is considered.\\




\subsection{Airfoil selection}
Based on the data obtained in the previous subsections, an airfoil from the NASA database was selected. The ARW-2 wing of the DAST drone used supercritical airfoils with varying thicknesses along the wing span. The profile thickness varied between $\frac{t}{c} = 0.14$ at the wing root to $\frac{t}{c} = 0.10$ at the wing tip. \cite{ARW-2_NASA} Based on the initial design lift coefficient $C_{L_\text{des}} = 0.2213$, the selected airfoil is based on a NASA report on supercritical airfoils. \cite{Harris1990} It was decided that the the NASA SC(2)-0414 will be used as the root wing profile, linearly transitioning towards the SC(2)-0412 profile at the wing tip. This means that the $\frac{t}{c}$ ratio is between 12 \% at the wing tip to 14 \% at the wing root.\\
The primary driver for selecting a relatively high thickness-to-chord ratio is the expected bending load on the slender wing of HUGO. The increased area moment of inertia of a thicker wing generally helps bending resistance. There are several risks associated with these aifoils with a relative high $\frac{t}{c}$ ratio. Compared to airfoils with higher $C_{L_\text{des}}$, flow separation and weak shock waves appear earlier, further from the trailing edge. However, at the same time, it has been reported that the overall drag divergence Mach number for airfoils with lower $C_{L_\text{des}}$ is generally higher. For the selected airfoil, the drag divergence Mach number $M_{dd}$ has been found to be around $M_{dd} = 0.8$, which is beyond the conditions HUGO will operate \cite{Harris1990}.

\chapter{Conclusion}

The midterm report aims to converge on a final HUGO configuration suitable for detailed design by establishing the operational, regulatory, and engineering foundations necessary to make the selection. The team has systematically evaluated five candidate architectures against HUGO's core mission requirements: the testing of aeroservoelastic phenomena on high aspect ratio wings at high subsonic speeds, within the constraints of certifiability, operability, and sustainability.

Den Helder Airport is confirmed as the base of operations, offering a controlled airspace environment, paved runway, and proximity to the designated North Sea test area. The certification pathway demonstrates that a Type Certificate supported by a Design Organisation Approval is both legally required and practically attainable, with the DOA mechanism allowing major structural reconfigurations to be classified as research procedures internally, avoiding full re-certification for each test campaign.

The engineering analyses in \autoref{ch:engineering_design} have yielded a set of preliminary design parameters. The NASA SC(2)-0012 airfoil was selected, with an estimated stall speed of approximately $37 \ m \cdot s^{-1}$ and a maximum lift coefficient of 1.22. Although possible limitations in the AeroSandbox software and potential issues with the model were observed, the results are considered sufficiently accurate for distinguishing between configurations, particularly at low angles of attack. The fuselage structural analysis demonstrates that the weight penalty of versatility features such as variable wing ports and canard ports is quantifiable and manageable. The flight envelope, constructed to EASA CS-23 aerobatic category requirements, shows that manoeuvre loads are the governing structural case, with gust loads remaining non-critical. The material and configuration trade-offs in \autoref{ch:trade-off} collectively indicate a clear design direction. Carbon fibre composite emerges as the primary structural material for the fuselage, wings, and empennage, while Aluminium 7075-T76 is preferred for localised high-stress components such as landing gear struts, pylons, and connection ports. The trade-off analysis eliminated HUG-CFG-304 for failing the cost no-go threshold, with HUG-CFG-303 emerging as the clear winner at a total score of 3.84 - a high-wing configuration with static wing and canard ports, and retractable landing gear selected for its balance of experimental versatility, cost efficiency, and reliability. The manufacturing, assembly, and integration plan in \autoref{ch:MAI} confirms that the selected material combination is feasible using established aerospace processes - prepreg autoclave curing for composites and CNC machining for metals - at the low production volumes expected for HUGO. A preliminary internal and external configuration layout has been established for HUG-CFG-303, and a lifecycle assessment confirms that the primary environmental impact arises from flight operations, with material production and end-of-life contributing comparatively minor shares.

Several assumptions limit the precision of the conclusions reached at this stage. Validation of the aerodynamic model against experimental data specific to HUGO was not possible due to the lack of available data, and known discrepancies with DATCOM predictions at higher angles of attack suggest that lift estimates may carry non-negligible uncertainty, particularly for the swept wing configuration. The structural weight penalty analysis relies on simplified beam models with point loads, which will require refinement once the final configuration geometry is fixed. These limitations are consistent with a midterm design stage and will be addressed systematically in the detailed design phase.

For the final report, the priority recommendations are: converging the weight and centre-of-gravity iteration loop, refining the aerodynamic model through higher-fidelity analysis at high subsonic conditions, completing the detailed structural design of the wing-fuselage and tail-fuselage static ports, and finalising the internal layout and fuel system sizing for HUG-CFG-303. On the organisational side, initiating preliminary contact with EASA regarding the DOA application and confirming hangar availability at Den Helder are advised as early actions that can be pursued in parallel with the ongoing design work.

\section{Noise Characteristics}
\label{sec:noise_characteristics}
The operation of the aircraft will inevitably lead to the generation of noise. Noise is small pressure differences which travels through a medium to the listener. This can be a great nuisance for the area surrounding the aircraft. Therefore it is important to obtain an understanding of the not only the magnitude but also at what frequencies the highest noise-level occurs.For an aircraft the primary source of noise would be the engine. This is especially true for turbine powered aircraft. Another source would be the aerodynamic surfaces and the fuselage itself. Finally the extended landing gear during take-off and approach. Each

The turbulent boundary layer of air over the different wing surfaces creates noise. The magnitude of the nose can be calculated using 
\begin{equation}
    OASPL = 50\log\left(\frac{V}{51.44}\right) + 10\log\left(\frac{\delta b}{h^2}\cos^2(\phi)\cos^2\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right) + 107.3
\end{equation}
V is the aircraft speed, b is the span of the aerodynamic surface (wing or tail), $\delta = 0.37 \frac{S}{b} \left(\frac{VS}{b\nu}\right)^{-0.2}$, h is the altitude of the aircraft, $\nu$ is the kinematic viscosity of the air, S is the area of the aerodynamic surface, $\phi$ and $\theta$ are the lateral and longitudinal angles relative to the observer.

Having all the different sources it is possible to add them together. 
\begin{equation}
    SPL_{total}=10 \log \sum^{i=N_{sources}}_{i=1}10^{\frac{SPL_i}{10}}
\end{equation} 
To accurate the noise perceived by a bystander distance must also be taken into account when calculating the noise. The aircraft is modeled as a point source with the sound uniformly propagates. This means that the sound pressure level will change based on the inverse square level:
\begin{equation}
    SPL_{r2}=SPL_{r_1}+20\log(\frac{r_1}{r_2})
\end{equation}
   

It has been chosen to specify the noise-spectrum  based on two different scenarios: 
The first being a flyover where the airplane starts somewhere in front of the observer and then passes over to continue behind the observer. For this analysis the height and the speed of the aircraft remains constant throughout the maneuver.

\section{Fuselage structural analysis}
The structural analysis of the fuselage has been conducted by considering the most constraining loading cases. Namely, the pull-up manoeuvre at the maximum load factor from the flight envelope, and the main landing gear impact for maximum vertical deceleration. For both of these, the fuselage has been discretised into a number of nodes, with each node being assigned a mass based on the estimated masses and positions of different components. The lift force of the canard is assumed to be a fraction of the total lift force:
\begin{equation}
    L_c=f\cdot L_{tot}
\end{equation}

In the first case, the aircraft is assumed to be accelerating vertically with a load factor $n$ and remaining in moment equilibrium.
The free-body and kinetic diagrams of the fuselage in flight, assuming detached canard and wing, are shown in \autoref{fig:fuselage_fbd_kd_flight}. The equations of motion are then

\begin{equation}
    \Sigma F: L_c+L_w+L_t-\Sigma m_i g = \Sigma m_i a_{cg}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,0) arc (-40:220:0.18)} \, \Sigma M_O: \quad -M_c - M_w + x_c L_c + x_w L_w + x_t L_t -\Sigma x_i m_i g  = 0
\end{equation}

where $a_{cg}=(n-1)g$.

In the second case, the aircraft is assumed to both accelerate vertically upwards due to landing impact, and rotate with a rotational acceleration about the center of gravity. The lift forces in this case are found by assuming the total lift equal to weight and in moment equilibrium with weight just before impact, with canard still assumed to contribute a fraction of the total lift. Hence, the equations of motion are

\begin{equation}
    \Sigma F: L_c+L_w+L_t+F_{lg}-\Sigma m_i g=\Sigma m_i a_{cg}
\end{equation}

and 

\begin{multline}
    \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,0) arc (-40:220:0.18)} \, \Sigma M_{cg}: \quad
    F_{lg}(x_{lg}-x_{cg}) + F_{brake}\,l_{lg} + L_t(x_t-x_{cg}) - L_w(x_{cg}-x_w) - M_w \\
    - L_c(x_{cg}-x_c) - M_c + \Sigma_i\,(x_i-x_{cg})\,m_i g = I_G\,\alpha
\end{multline}

Then, accelerations of particular nodes are given by 
\begin{equation}
    a_i=a_{cg}+\alpha (x_i-x_{cg})
\end{equation}

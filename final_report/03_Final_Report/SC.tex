\section{Fuselage structural analysis}
\label{sec:fuselage_structural_analysis}
The structural analysis of the fuselage has been conducted by considering the most constraining loading cases. Namely, the pull-up manoeuvre at the maximum load factor from the flight envelope, and the main landing gear impact for maximum vertical deceleration. For both of these, the fuselage has been discretised into $n$ nodes, with each node being assigned a mass based on the estimated masses (both structural and non-structural) and positions of different components. The lift force of the canard is assumed to be a fraction of the total lift force:
\begin{equation}
    L_c=f\cdot L_{tot}
\end{equation}

In the first case, the aircraft is assumed to be accelerating vertically with a load factor $n$ and remaining in moment equilibrium.
The free-body and kinetic diagrams of the fuselage in flight, assuming detached canard and wing, are shown in \autoref{fig:fuselage_fbd_kd_flight}. The equations of motion are then

\begin{equation}
     \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,-0.25) -- (0,0.25);}
    \quad
    \Sigma F: L_c+L_w+L_t-\Sigma_{i=0}^n m_i g = \Sigma_{i=0}^n m_i a_{cg}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,0) arc (-40:220:0.18)} \, \Sigma M_O: \quad -M_c - M_w + x_c L_c + x_w L_w + x_t L_t -\Sigma_{i=0}^n x_i m_i g  = 0
\end{equation}

where $a_{cg}=(n-1)g$.

In the second case, the aircraft is assumed to both accelerate vertically upwards due to landing impact, and rotate with a rotational acceleration about the center of gravity. The lift forces in this case are found by assuming the total lift equal to weight and in moment equilibrium with weight just before impact, with canard still assumed to contribute a fraction of the total lift. Hence, the equations of motion are

\begin{equation}
     \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,-0.25) -- (0,0.25);}
    \quad \Sigma F: L_c+L_w+L_t+F_{lg}-\Sigma m_i g=\Sigma m_i a_{cg}
\end{equation}

and 

\begin{multline}
    \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,0) arc (-40:220:0.18)} \, \Sigma M_{cg}: \quad
    F_{lg}(x_{lg}-x_{cg}) + F_{brake}\,l_{lg} + L_t(x_t-x_{cg}) - L_w(x_{cg}-x_w) - M_w \\
    - L_c(x_{cg}-x_c) - M_c + \Sigma_i\,(x_i-x_{cg})\,m_i g = I_G\,\alpha
\end{multline}

where $l_{lg}$ is the length of the landing gear strut. Then, the acceleration of a particular node is given by 
\begin{equation}
    a(x_i)=a_{cg}+\alpha (x_i-x_{cg})
\end{equation}

Finally, the internal shear force at node $i$ can then be found as 
\begin{equation}
    \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,0.25) -- (0,-0.25);}
    \quad
    V(x_i)=\Sigma_{j=0}^i \left(F_{\text{applied},j}-m_j g-m_j a_j\right)
\end{equation}
% \begin{equation}
%     V(x_i)=\Sigma_{j=0}^i (F_{\text{applied},j} - m_j g -  m_j a_j)
% \end{equation}

and the internal bending moment as 

\begin{equation}
    \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,0) arc (220:-40:0.18)} \quad M(x_i)=\Sigma_{j=0}^i(M_{\text{applied},j}+(x_i-x_j)(F_{\text{applied},j}-m_j(g+a(x_j))))
\end{equation}

Once the structural analysis of the fuselage has been completed, the design can proceed to analyse the structural properties of the wing. The aerodynamic platform is analysed in both the most critical conditions, during landing when the force of the impact multiplies the weight, during take off conditions when the weight is at its maximum and the wing experiences maximum lift loads and lastly cruise. 

This section focuses on deriving a method applicable for all wing profiles given to HUGO, to compute the structural characteristics such as internal shear, internal bending moments, torsion, deflection, wing rotation and failure determination during normal operation.

\section{Fuselage structural analysis}
\label{sec:fuselage_structural_analysis}
The structural analysis of the fuselage has been conducted by considering the most constraining loading cases. Namely, the pull-up manoeuvre at the maximum load factor from the flight envelope, and the main landing gear impact for maximum vertical deceleration. For both of these, the fuselage has been discretised into $n$ nodes, with each node being assigned a mass based on the estimated masses (both structural and non-structural) and positions of different components. The lift force of the canard is assumed to be a fraction of the total lift force:
\begin{equation}
    L_c=f\cdot L_{tot}
\end{equation}

In the first case, the aircraft is assumed to be accelerating vertically due to a load factor $n$ and remaining in moment equilibrium.
The free-body and kinetic diagrams of the fuselage in flight, assuming detached canard and wing, are shown in \autoref{fig:fuselage_fbd_kd_flight}. The equations of motion are then

\begin{equation}
     \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,-0.25) -- (0,0.25);}
    \quad
    \Sigma F: L_c+L_w+L_t-\Sigma_{i=0}^n m_i g = \Sigma_{i=0}^n m_i a_{i}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,0) arc (-40:220:0.18)} \, \Sigma M_{cg}: \quad -M_c - M_w + x_c L_c + x_w L_w + x_t L_t -\Sigma_{i=0}^n x_i m_i g  = \Sigma_{i=0}^n x_i m_i a_i
\end{equation}

where $a_i=a_{cg}=(n-1)g$.

In the second case, the aircraft is assumed to both accelerate vertically upwards due to landing impact, and rotate with a rotational acceleration about the center of gravity. The lift forces in this case are found by assuming the total lift equal to weight and in moment equilibrium with weight just before impact, with canard still assumed to contribute a fraction of the total lift. The free-body and kinetic diagrams at the instant of the impact are shown in \autoref{fig:fuselage_fbd_kd_landing}. Hence, the equations of motion are

\begin{equation}
     \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,-0.25) -- (0,0.25);}
    \quad \Sigma F: L_c+L_w+L_t+F_{lg}-\Sigma m_i g=\Sigma m_i a_{cg}
\end{equation}

and 

\begin{multline}
    \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,0) arc (-40:220:0.18)} \, \Sigma M_{cg}: \quad
    F_{lg}(x_{lg}-x_{cg}) + F_{brake}\,l_{lg} + L_t(x_t-x_{cg}) - L_w(x_{cg}-x_w) - M_w \\
    - L_c(x_{cg}-x_c) - M_c + \Sigma_i\,(x_i-x_{cg})\,m_i g = I_G\,\alpha
\end{multline}

where $l_{lg}$ is the length of the landing gear strut. Then, the acceleration of a particular node is given by 
\begin{equation}
    a(x_i)=a_{cg}+\alpha (x_i-x_{cg})
\end{equation}

Finally, the internal shear force at node $i$ can then be found as 
\begin{equation}
    \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,0.25) -- (0,-0.25);}
    \quad
    V(x_i)=\Sigma_{j=0}^i \left(F_{\text{applied},j}-m_j g-m_j a_j\right)
\end{equation}
% \begin{equation}
%     V(x_i)=\Sigma_{j=0}^i (F_{\text{applied},j} - m_j g -  m_j a_j)
% \end{equation}

and the internal bending moment as 

\begin{equation}
    \tikz[baseline=-0.6ex]{\draw[->] (0,0) arc (220:-40:0.18)} \quad M(x_i)=\Sigma_{j=0}^i(M_{\text{applied},j}+(x_i-x_j)(F_{\text{applied},j}-m_j(g+a(x_j))))
\end{equation}

Once the structural analysis of the fuselage has been completed, the design can proceed to analyse the structural properties of the wing. The aerodynamic platform is analysed under the most critical conditions: during landing, when the impact force multiplies the weight, during take-off, when the weight is at its maximum and the wing experiences maximum lift loads; and, lastly, in cruise. 

This section focuses on deriving a method applicable for all wing profiles given to HUGO, to compute the structural characteristics such as internal shear, internal bending moments, torsion, deflection, wing rotation and failure determination during normal operation. For the purpose of this analysis, the foam interior is not taken into account for the structural calculations, leaving only the composite skin as the prime structural element.

From the requirements, especially HUG-STR-07, it has been determined that the extreme loading scenario of the wing planform occurs during the loading manoeuvre that stresses the airframe up to 6 times the normal amount. During landing, the impact would produce an inertial load of 4, smaller then the previous 6, and in cruise conditions, the load factor remains at 1. Taking this into account, for the purpose of this model, only the extreme loading case will be analysed.
% \begin{itemize}
%     \item Maneuver loading at cruise conditions and load factor 6 (HUG-TPL-04) and angle of attack $8 \deg$,
%     \item Cruise and Test conditions at load factor 1 and angle of attack $1 \deg$
%     \item Landing conditions at weight load factor is 4, angle of attack $10 \deg$, at $50 m/s$.
    
% \end{itemize}

As introduced in \autoref{sec}, the structural model is based on the NASA SC(2)-0012 airfoil shown in \autoref{fig:airfoil_simplified_model}. This symmetric supercritical airfoil is used as the reference geometry for the wing sections analysed in the structural tool. To simplify the analytical model, each wing section is approximated as a hollow ellipse with the same chord and maximum thickness as the airfoil. The lift resultant is applied at the quarter-chord point, consistent with the typical location of the aerodynamic centre for subsonic airfoils\footnote{\href{http://airfoiltools.com/airfoil/details?airfoil=sc20012-il}{NASA SC(2)-0012 airfoil data} (accessed on 10.06.2026).}.

This elliptical approximation increases the estimated second moment of area compared with a direct airfoil-skin representation. However, the model also neglects the structural contribution of the foam core. The resulting section properties are therefore treated as simplified equivalent properties for preliminary structural sizing. For this analysis, the wing will be treated as a beam fixed at one end, deformations and deflections being based on Euler–Bernoulli beam theory as a first-order approximation.

In \autoref{sec:fuselage_structural_analysis}, the fuselage was divided into individual nodes and treated independently. The same approach is applied to the wing, where the half-span is divided into $n$ nodes, each treated as a wing section. The resolution is left up to the user, each part having its own weight, lift and moment contribution. To get the forces acting on the entire wing, the perimeter, area and moment of inertia need to be calculated for each individual wing increment. 

Since the airfoil is modelled as an ellipse, the perimeter of the sections is computed using Ramanujan's second equation for area \footnote{\href{https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perimeter_of_an_ellipse}{Perimeter of an ellipse} (accessed on 10.06.2026)} \autoref{eq:perim_area}, where $a$ and $b$ are the semi-major and semi-minor axes of the ellipse. 

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
h &= \left(\frac{a-b}{a+b}\right)^2
\qquad\qquad
P &\approx \pi(a+b)
\left[
1 + \frac{3h}{10 + \sqrt{4 - 3h}}
\right]
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:perim_area}
\end{equation}

The structural analysis is formulated with several parameters left available for parametric sizing, including the wing skin thickness. To determine the wing response under applied loads, the cross-sectional material area is required. This area is computed using the method provided by Niu \cite[p.~765]{niu1999airframe}. The same source is used for the expression of the second moment of area of a hollow ellipse.

For the structural analysis of HUGO's wings, a computational tool was developed to evaluate the structural response of different planforms during normal operation. The tool estimates the applied torque, wing-tip deflection, wing-tip rotation, internal shear forces, internal shear stresses, and the buckling behaviour of the wing. These outputs are used to verify that the wing structure remains within acceptable limits throughout the operating envelope, including extra loads experienced during take-off or landing. 

Each section is exposed to a different lift and weight, depending on the location from the root. Lift division is evaluated in \autoref{sec:lift}, while for the weight equation \autoref{eq:section_weight} is used. \begin{equation} W = perimeter \cdot t_{skin} \cdot l_{section} \cdot 9.81 \label{eq:section_weight} \end{equation} \begin{wrapfigure}[13]{r}{0.4\textwidth} \centering \includegraphics[width=0.43\textwidth]{figures_final/Force distribution.png} \caption{Force distribution during cruise conditions and load factor 1} \label{fig:force_distribution} \end{wrapfigure} Cases such as wing deflection or internal stresses require a net force obtained by subtracting the wing weight from the lift for each segment. The resulting force distribution is shown in \autoref{fig:force_distribution}.

For the chosen airfoil, the torque is given by the combination of the lift produced by the wing and the aerodynamic moment of the airfoil itself. The torque is computed by multiplying the choarter chord length by the lift of the section, and then summing the contributions one by one until the root chord is reached \autoref{eq:t_sec_cumulative}, where $i$ is the previous segment.
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:t_sec_cumulative}
\begin{aligned}
    T_{\mathrm{sec}} &= L_{\mathrm{sec}} \cdot \frac{\mathrm{chord}}{4}
    \qquad &
    T_{i+1} &= \sum_{j=0}^{i} T_j
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

At $1 \deg $ angle of attack, the airfoil has a moment coefficient that tends towards 0\footnote{\label{ft:NASA_0012}\href{http://airfoiltools.com/polar/details?polar=xf-sc20012-il-500000-n5}{NASA_0012} (accessed on 10.06.2026)} as such, for the cruise and test conditions, the torque graphs will look the same. The most critical case for the torque of the wing comes from the first case specified at the beginning of the chapter at a load factor of 6, with the distribution presented in \autoref{fig:torsion_maneuver}.

Torque will influence the twist of the wing tip, but to determine the deflection of the wing it is first required to calculate the internal forces and moments acting on the wing. The wing is exposed to forces originating from the lift and weight, acting tangential to the chord, with the exception of the normal forces introduced by bending that will be treated in the following.

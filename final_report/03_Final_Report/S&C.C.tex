\section{Horizontal Stabilizer Sizing}
\label{sec:hor_stabilizer_sizing}

To satisfy HUG-STB-01, the horizontal stabiliser should cause stability close to marginal for the default value of the CG (when the fuel is distributed equally between the tanks), so that fuel movement either to the front or aft causes the CG excursion required for the stability regime of interest. This can be achieved when sizing a tail to the minimum area required for the combination of stability and controllability. For canard configurations, however, increasing the stabiliser size improves controllability, decreasing stability at the same time, which makes the condition both stability and controllability more challenging to achieve. As such, it is decided to demonstrate compliance with HUG-STB-01 using a t-tail empennage. Consequently, configurations containing a canard are sized for controllability, while configurations not containing one are sized for both stability and controllability. 

\subsection{Horizontal Stabiliser Sizing using Semi-Empirical Formulae}
\label{sub:semi-empirical-tail}
Assuming constant aerodynamic coefficients, \autoref{eq:sh/s1} and \autoref{eq:sh/s2} can be derived for the surface areas required for stability $(S_h/S)_{\text{min, st}}$ and controllability $(S_h/S)_{\text{min, ct}}$, respectively.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sh/s1}
    \left(\frac{S_h}{S}\right)_{\text{min, st}} = \frac{\bar{x}_{{cg}_\text{max}}-\bar{x}_{ac} + 0.05}{\frac{C_{L\alpha_h}}{C_{L\alpha_{A-h}}} \left( 1 - \frac{d\varepsilon}{d\alpha} \right) \frac{l_h}{\bar{c}} \left( \frac{V_h}{V} \right)^2} 
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sh/s2}
    \left(\frac{S_h}{S}\right)_{\text{min, ct}} = \left|\frac{\bar{x}_{{cg}_\text{min}} + \frac{C_{m_{ac}}}{C_{L_{A-h}}} - \bar{x}_{ac}}{\frac{C_{L_h}}{C_{L_{A-h}}} \frac{l_h}{\bar{c}} \left( \frac{V_h}{V} \right)^2}\right|
\end{equation}

Here, 0.05 is a common value of stability margin selected during aircraft preliminary design \cite{stat-stab-margin}, and $\bar{x}_{{cg}_\text{min}}$, $\bar{x}_{{cg}_\text{max}}$ are the most fore and aft CG positions of the entire aircraft, respectively. $\bar{x}_{ac}$ is the position of the aerodynamic centre of the aircraft-less-horizontal-stabiliser (approximated by main wing aerodynamic centre) as a fraction of the MAC, $l_h/\bar c$ is the distance between horizontal stabiliser and main wing aerodynamic centres, $V_h/V$ is the ratio of airspeeds at the horizontal stabiliser to those at the main wing (equal to one for both canard and t-tail; due to their low interference with the main wing, effectively clean airflow is seen by those stabiliser configurations).
$C_{L_{\alpha_h}}$ and $C_{L_{h}}$ are the lift slope and maximum lift coefficient of the stabiliser, while $C_{L_{\alpha_{A-h}}}$ and $C_{L_{A-h}}$ are those of aircraft-less-horizontal-stabiliser, approximated by those of the main wing. 

It shall be noted that $x_{cg_\text{min}}$ and  $x_{cg_\text{max}}$ also correspond to the CG locations of the UAV with tanks fully fuelled and with zero fuel, respectively. Pumping fuel between the fuel bladders  allows the operators to exceed that CG range and hence, enter an unstable flight regime. The lift slopes are computed using DATCOM \footnote{\href{https://www.fzt.haw-hamburg.de/pers/Scholz/HOOU/AircraftDesign_7_WingDesign.pdf}{Scholz} (accessed on 16.06.2026)}. 

For the standard planform, the fuel tanks are positioned approximately symmetrically with respect to the global CG, resulting in $x_{{cg}_\text{min}}$, $x_{{cg}_\text{max}}$ being equal to 1.414 m and 1.416 m measured from the nose. The resultant CG excursion between the fully fuelled and zero fuel results in a tail surface ratio $S_h/S$ of 0.106 and a total aircraft mass of 46.76 kg.

The choice of horizontal stabiliser is left to the client as a configuration choice. However, the aspect ratio of the horizontal stabiliser will impact the total mass in the standard planform configuration independent of the client's choice between a conventional tail or canard. \autoref{fig:sensitivity-h-stab} shows the effect of changing the aspect ratio of both a conventional tail (blue) and canard (orange) on the total aircraft mass in the standard planform configuration.

    \caption{Effect of horizontal stabilizer aspect ratio on the total mass in

The trend for both options of horizontal stabiliser is to show a diminishing decrease of MTOM with increasing aspect ratio. The only deviation is at an aspect ratio of approximately 9 for the conventional tail, resulting in a sharp upwards spike. This is attributed to discrete thickness steps used in the planform structural sizing. From an aspect ratio of 9 onwards, the tail needs to be at the next thickness step.

Overall, for the examined range of aspect ratios, the MTOM does not vary by more than 1 kg, indicating a low sensitivity of the design on the horizontal stabiliser aspect ratio.

This software uses a potential flow solver, viscosity and wake models for preliminary estimation of aircraft characteristics. These include lift distribution, drag estimation, static stability and dynamic stability analysis. \footnote{\label{ft:flow5}\href{https://flow5.tech/docs/flow5_doc/flow5_doc.html}{flow5 methods overview}(Accessed on 08.06.2026)} The aircraft was modeled and meshed in this software using the fuselage CAD model and a built-in wing generation tool, which was used for the main wing, tail and canard surfaces. The chosen configuration for analysis includes a standard wing and T-tail defined in \autoref{sec:external_configuration}, without a canard attached. Furthermore, the mass distribution and inertial properties were reconstructed in flow5 based on the HUGO CAD model and the determined configuration layout. \subsection{Ailerons}
Based on the collected information, the roll characteristics were initially tested on the HUGO reference wing planform with ailerons having a 20 \% chord-to-length ratio, spanning 25 \% of the half-wing span and located at mid-wing. These control surfaces were tested in an approximate approach speed regime, where $V_{appr} \approx 50 \ m \cdot s^{-1}$ at an angle of attack $\alpha=7.5 \degree$. The air used in the analysis had a zero-altitude ISA density, i.e. $\rho = 1.225 \ kg \cdot m^-3$. Each aileron has been set to 20 \degree deflection with opposing deflection direction on each side. At this flight velocity, the maximum calculated hinge moment on a single aileron is $0.084 \ N\cdot m$, with both ailerons deflected in opposite directions. Note that the aileron is divided into two flaps in \autoref{fig:flow5_ailerons}. The resulting roll rate p is calculated using \autoref{eq:roll_rate} for steady-state roll rate \cite{nelson1998flight}.
\begin{equation}
    p = -2 \frac{V}{b} \frac{C_{l_{\delta_{a}}}}{C_{l_{p}}}\delta_a
    \label{eq:roll_rate}
\end{equation}
Here, $V = V_{appr}$ is the approach velocity, $b = 3.4\ m$ is the wing span, $C_{l_{\delta_{a}}}$ is the control derivative representing the change of $C_l$ with aileron deflection $\delta_a$ and $C_{l_{p}} = -0.4746$ is the stability derivative of the change of $C_l$ with the roll rate $p$ found using flow5. The only unknown parameter is the change of the roll moment coefficient with aileron deflection $C_{l_{\delta_{a}}}$. This parameter was calculated at the approach speed $V_{appr} = 50 \ m \cdot s^{-1}$ from the change of $C_l$ between two aileron deflections $\delta_a = 0 \degree$ and $\delta_a = \pm0.1 \degree$. This resulted in $C_{l_{\delta_{a}}} = -0.127 \ rad^{-1}$. The roll rate $p$ at different aileron deflections $\delta_{a}$ are shown in \autoref{tab:delta_a_alpha}.
\begin{table}[H]

To estimate the aileron moment loads at the target experiment flight speed of approximately $230 \ m \cdot s^{-1}$ at FL270 ISA altitude, it has been set that the maximum roll rate defined in the approach regime shall be achieved. The required aileron deflection to reach the required roll rate has been found to be $\delta_a = \pm3.89 \degree$ using \autoref{eq:roll_rate}. flow5 outputs a moment $H_a = 0.1791 \ N \cdot m$ for a single aileron at this speed regime. This speed lies in compressibility region, therefore accounting for this in the hinge moment is needed. The value obtained in flow5 was then scaled using the Prandtl-Glauert compressibility correction in \autoref{eq:ch_compressibility}, according to the method proposed by Simpson \cite{Simpson2016}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures_final/flow5_ailerons_approach.png}
    \caption{Ailerons with $\delta_a = \pm 20 \degree$ at $V_{appr}$. The color scale represents the $\gamma$ parameter representing the doublet densities and vortex strengths in flow5 \footref{ft:flow5}}
    \label{fig:flow5_ailerons}
\end{figure}

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ch_compressibility}
    H_{a_{c}} = \frac{H_a}{\sqrt{1-M^2}} = \frac{0.1791}{\sqrt{1-0.75^2}} \approx0.2708 \ N\cdot m
\end{equation}
The aileron hinge moment at the experiment flight condition $H_{a_{c}}$ is important for the structural design of the aileron structure, actuator links and selecting the appropriate actuator size.


\subsection{Aileron analysis verification}
\label{sec:aileron_vnv}
To ensure the results obtained for the ailerons sized with flow5, Sadraey's aileron sizing methodology was used \cite[p.657-661]{sadraey2012aircraftdesign}. To do so, the roll moment coefficients due to aileron deflection and roll rate had to be approximated. This was done using \autoref{eq:roll_aileron} and \autoref{eq:roll_roll}.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roll_aileron}
    C_{l_{\delta_{a}}} = 
    \frac{2 C_{l_{\alpha}} \tau}{S_{\text{ref}}\, b} \int_{b_1}^{b_2} c(y)\, y\, dy
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roll_roll}
    C_{l_{p}} = 
    -\frac{4(C_{l_{\alpha}} + C_{d_{0}})}{S_{\text{ref}}\, b^2}\int_{0}^{b/2} y^2\, c(y)\, dy
\end{equation}
where $C_{l_{\alpha}}$ is the airfoil lift curve slope, $\tau$ is the aileron effectiveness, $c(y)$ is the chord along the wing span, $y$ is the position along the wing span, $b_1$ and $b_2$ are the start and end positions of the ailerons, $C_{d_{0}}$ is the zero lift drag, and $S_{\text{ref}}$ is the wetted area of the wing.

The aileron effectiveness curve takes the wing to aileron chord ratio as an input. Unfortunately, Sadraey only provides a graphical interpretation of the curve without an accompanying function. To keep the aileron sizing function parametric, points on the curve were selected and \texttt{scipy.interpolate} was used to fit a logarithmic curve described by \autoref{eq:tau_fit}.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau_fit}
    \tau(x) = a \ln(bx + 1) + c
\end{equation}
Here, $a = 0.43944$, $b = 7.35977$, and $c = 2.452 \cdot 10^{-4}$.

Using \autoref{eq:roll_rate}, the roll rate can then be calculated and compared to the value obtained in flow5. It was observed that given the same geometric parameters as flow5, the final roll rate was off by 24\%. Comparing the individual $C_{l_{p}}$ and $C_{l_{\delta_{a}}}$ to those from flow5, an error of 61\% and 21\% were found respectively. Though Sadraey does not report the accuracy of his methods, the discrepancy can likely be attributed to the fact flow5 uses a three-dimensional panel method, whereas Sadraey uses a simplified analytical integration of lift distribution spanwise. The Sadrey methods are thus better understood as an order of accuracy test, which qualitatively agree with the results obtained in flow5 despite the rather large difference in $C_{l_p}$.%obtained roll rate.

\section{Dynamic characteristics of the aircraft}
\label{sec:dynamic_stab}
Eigenmotions of HUGO were studied in order to assess the dynamic behaviour of the aircraft within its flight envelope. Both longitudinal and lateral eigenmotions were investigated for the canard and non-canard configuration using the flow5 software. The aircraft properties were transferred from the CAD model and included identical geometric, mass and inertial properties. \autoref{fig:flow5_overview} shows the aircraft properties used in this simulation. \autoref{fig:longitudinal_dynamic_modes_cruise} and \autoref{fig:longitudinal_dynamic_modes_approach} show the modal responses in the longitudinal stability direction with the horizontal velocity disturbance $u$, vertical velocity disturbance $w$, pitch angle $\theta$ and pitch rate $q$. \autoref{fig:lateral_directional_dynamic_modes_cruise} and \autoref{fig:lateral_directional_dynamic_modes_approach} show characteristic modes for the lateral stability direction with lateral velocity disturbance $v$, roll rate $p$, yaw rate $r$ and bank angle $\varphi$. 

The modal response plots were not interpreted as physical aircraft attitude or rate amplitudes. In a linear modal analysis, each mode is represented by an eigenvector, whose magnitude is arbitrary. Multiplying the eigenvector by any scalar gives the same mode behaviour. flow5 does not allow for scaling the eigenvector magnitudes, therefore the unscaled eigenmode responses are shown as plotted by the software. \footnote{\href{https://flow5.tech/docs/flow5_doc/Tutorials/Stability.html}{flow5 Stability Analysis}(Accessed 16.06.2026)}

This chapter has shown that the HUGO configuration derived in the preliminary design phase is both stable and controllable across the intended flight envelope. The aircraft maintains adequate static stability margins while providing sufficient control authority for trim and manoeuvring in all analysed flight conditions.

The dynamic analysis confirms that all dominant modes remain stable, with no critical instabilities observed. Although the phugoid mode in cruise is relatively weakly damped, it remains within acceptable limits for the intended operation.

Overall, the results validate the stability and control assumptions made in the preliminary design summary and confirm that the selected configuration is feasible from both a handling and controllability perspective.

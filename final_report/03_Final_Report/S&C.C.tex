The purpose of the stability and control analysis is to verify that HUGO can remain controllable during both low-speed operations and the high-speed test segment, while accommodating the mass distribution and configuration changes. The analysis therefore focuses on control surface authority, actuator hinge moment requirements, and the static and dynamic stability characteristics over the relevant CG range. 




\section{Tail Sizing}

\subsection{Effect of Horizontal Stabilizer Aspect Ratio on Total Mass in
the Standard Planform Configuration}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\linewidth]{figures_final/sensitivity-study/empennage aspect ratio.png}
    \caption{Effect of horizontal stabilizer aspect ratio on the total mass in
the standard planform configuration}
    \label{fig:sensitivity-h-stab}
\end{figure}

The choice of horizontal stabiliser is left to the client as a configuration choice. However, the aspect ratio of the horizontal stabiliser will impact the total mass in the standard planform configuration independent of the client's choice between a conventional tail or canard. \autoref{fig:sensitivity-h-stab} shows the effect of changing the aspect ratio of both a conventional tail (blue) and canard (orange) on the total aircraft mass in the standard planform configuration. 

The trend for both options of horizontal stabiliser is to show a diminishing decrease of MTOM with increasing aspect ratio. The only deviation is at an aspect ratio of approximately 9 for the conventional tail, resulting in a sharp upwards spike. This is attributed to discrete thickness steps used in the planform structural sizing. From an aspect ratio of 9 onwards, the tail needs to be at the next thickness step.

Overall, for the examined range of aspect ratios, the MTOM does not vary by more than 1 kg, indicating a low sensitivity of the design on the horizontal stabiliser aspect ratio.

% Both for the canard or the t-tail, increasing the aspect ratio offers diminishing returns in terms of total aircraft mass. Larger variation in magnitudes, as well MTOM increasing around the aspect ratio of 9, are observed for the t-tail. This is expected, as for the t-tail there is the downwash from the main wing, which affects tails of different aspect ratios differently \cite{downwashFormula}, creating an additional, highly chaotic aspect ratio dependency compared to the canard.

\section{Control Moments}
\label{sec:control_forces}
Recently re-developed and updated software, Flow5, is used to demonstrate HUGO's capability of operation in various flight regimes and outputs several important values for the design, such as control surface actuator moments and eigenmotion characteristics. The forces and moments on the control surfaces were calculated using the Flow5 software on a reference wing and tail planform from \autoref{tab:standard_wing_planform}. This software uses a potential flow solver, viscosity and wake models for preliminary estimation of aircraft characteristics. These include lift distribution, drag estimation, static stability and dynamic stability analysis. \footnote{\href{https://flow5.tech/docs/flow5_doc/flow5_doc.html}{Flow5 methods overview}(Accessed on 08.06.2026)} The aircraft was modeled and meshed in this software using the fuselage CAD model and a built-in wing generation tool, which was used for the main wing, tail and canard surfaces. The chosen configuration for analysis includes a standard wing and T-tail defined in \autoref{}, without a canard attached. Furthermore, the mass distribution and inertial properties were reconstructed in Flow5 based on the HUGO CAD model and the determined configuration layout.

\subsection{Ailerons}
Based on the collected information, the roll characteristics were initially tested on the HUGO reference wing planform with ailerons having a 20 \% chord-to-length ratio, spanning 25 \% of the half-wing span and located at mid-wing. These control surfaces were tested in an approximate approach speed regime, where $V_{appr} \approx 50 \ m \cdot s^{-1}$ at an angle of attack $\alpha=7.5 \degree$. The air used in the analysis had a zero-altitude ISA density, i.e. $\rho = 1.225 \ kg \cdot m^-3$. Each aileron has been set to 20 \degree deflection with opposing deflection direction on each side. At this flight velocity, the maximum calculated hinge moment on a single aileron is $0.084 \ N\cdot m$, with both ailerons deflected in opposite directions. Note that the aileron is divided into two flaps in \autoref{fig:flow5_ailerons}. The resulting roll rate p is calculated using \autoref{eq:roll_rate} for steady-state roll rate \cite{nelson1998flight}.
\begin{equation}
    p = -2 \frac{V}{b} \frac{C_{l_{\delta_{a}}}}{C_{l_{p}}}\delta_a
    \label{eq:roll_rate}
\end{equation}
Here, $V = V_{appr}$, $b = 3.4\ m$ is the wing span, $C_{l_{\delta_{a}}}$ is the control derivative representing the change of $C_l$ with aileron deflection $\delta_a$ and $C_{l_{p}} = -0.4746$ is the stability derivative of the change of $C_l$ with the roll rate $p$ found using Flow5. The only unknown parameter is the change of the roll moment coefficient with aileron deflection $C_{l_{\delta_{a}}}$. This parameter was calculated at the approach speed $V_{appr} = 50 \ m \cdot s^{-1}$ from the change of $C_l$ between two aileron deflections $\delta_a = 0 \degree$ and $\delta_a = \pm0.1 \degree$. This resulted in $C_{l_{\delta_{a}}} = -0.127 \ rad^{-1}$. The roll rate $p$ at different aileron deflections $\delta_{a}$ are shown in \autoref{tab:delta_a_alpha}.

To estimate the aileron moment loads at the target experiment flight speed of $M = 0.75 \approx 230 \ m \cdot s^{-1}$ at FL270 ISA altitude, it has been set that the maximum roll rate, achieved in the approach regime, shall be achieved. The required aileron deflection to reach the required roll rate has been found to be $\delta_a = \pm3.89 \degree$ using \autoref{eq:roll_rate}. Flow5 outputs a moment $H_a = 0.1791 \ N \cdot m$ for a single aileron at this speed regime. This speed lies in compressibility region, therefore accounting for this in the hinge moment is needed. Using the expression in \autoref{eq:hinge_moment} \cite{}, value obtained in Flow5 was then scaled using the Prandtl-Glauert compressibility correction in \autoref{eq:ch_compressibility}, analogous to the method Simpson used \cite{Simpson2016}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures_final/Flow5_ailerons_approach.png}
    \caption{Ailerons with $\delta_a = \pm 20 \degree$ at $V_{appr}$. The color scale represents the $\gamma$ parameter representing the doublet densities and vortex strengths in Flow5 \cite{}}
    \label{fig:flow5_ailerons}
\end{figure}

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ch_compressibility}
    H_{a_{c}} = \frac{H_a}{\sqrt{1-M^2}} = \frac{0.1791}{\sqrt{1-0.75^2}} \approx0.2708 \ N\cdot m
\end{equation}
The aileron hinge moment at the experiment flight condition $H_{a_{c}}$ is important for the structural design of the aileron structure, actuator links and selecting the appropriate actuator size.


\subsection{Aileron analysis verification}
To ensure the results obtained for the ailerons sized with Flow5, Sadraey's aileron sizing methodology was used (as described in the ADSEE-II course). To do so, the roll moment coefficients due to aileron deflection and roll rate had to be approximated. This was done using \autoref{eq:roll_aileron} and \autoref{eq:roll_roll}.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roll_aileron}
    C_{l_{\delta_{a}}} = 
    \frac{2 C_{l_{\alpha}} \tau}{S_{\text{ref}}\, b} \int_{b_1}^{b_2} c(y)\, y\, dy
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:roll_roll}
    C_{l_{p}} = 
    -\frac{4(C_{l_{\alpha}} + C_{d_{0}})}{S_{\text{ref}}\, b^2}\int_{0}^{b/2} y^2\, c(y)\, dy
\end{equation}
where $C_{l_{\alpha}}$ is the airfoil lift curve slope, $\tau$ is the aileron effectiveness, $c(y)$ is the chord along the wing span, $y$ is the position along the wing span, $b_1$ and $b_2$ are the start and end positions of the ailerons, $C_{d_{0}}$ is the zero lift drag, and $S_{\text{ref}}$ is the wetted area of the wing.

The aileron effectiveness curve takes the wing to aileron chord ratio as an input. Unfortunately Sadraey only provides this graph with no accompanying function. To keep the aileron sizing function parametric, points on the curve were selected and \texttt{scipy.interpolate} was used to fit a logarithmic curve as shown in \autoref{eq:tau_fit},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tau_fit}
    \tau = a \ln(bx + 1) + c
\end{equation}
where $a = 0.43944$, $b = 7.35977$, and $c = 2.452 \times 10^{-4}$.

Using \autoref{eq:roll_rate}, the roll rate can then be calculated and compared to the value obtained in Flow5. It was observed that given the same geometric parameters as Flow5, the final roll rate was off by 24\%. Comparing the individual $C_{l_{p}}$ and $C_{l_{\delta_{a}}}$ to those from Flow5, an error of 61\% and 21\% were found respectively. Though Sadraey does not report the accuracy of his methods, the discrepancy can likely be attributed to the fact Flow5 uses \todo{what} whereas Sadraey uses a linearised model, excluding higher order terms. Additionally, Sadraey does not take wing sweep into account, needing an adaptation to this formulas by taking the velocity times the cosine of the quarter chord sweep. The Sadrey methods are thus better understood as an order of accuracy test, which agree with the results obtained in Flow5 despite the rather large difference in obtained roll rate.

\section{Dynamic characteristics of the aircraft}
Eigenmotions of HUGO were studied in order to assess the dynamic behavior of the aircraft within its flight envelope. Both longitudinal and lateral eigenmotions were investigated for both the canard and non-canard configuration using the Flow5 software. The aircraft properties were transferred from the CAD model and included identical geometric, mass and inertial properties. \autoref{fig:flow5_overview} shows the aircraft properties used in this simulation. \autoref{fig:longitudinal_dynamic_modes} shows the modal responses in the longitudinal stability direction with the horizontal velocity disturbance $u$, vertical velocity disturbance $w$, pitch angle $\theta$ and pitch rate $q$. \autoref{fig:lateral_directional_dynamic_modes} shows characteristic modes for the lateral stability direction with lateral velocity disturbance $v$, roll rate $p$, yaw rate $r$ and bank angle $\varphi$. The modal response plots were not interpreted as physical aircraft attitude or rate amplitudes. In a linear modal analysis, each mode is represented by an eigenvector, whose magnitude is arbitrary. Multiplying the eigenvector by any scalar gives the same mode behavior. Flow5 does not allow for scaling the eigenvector magnitudes, therefore the unscaled eigenmode responses are shown as plotted by the software. \footnote{\href{https://flow5.tech/docs/flow5_doc/Tutorials/Stability.html}{Flow5 Stability Analysis}(Accessed 16.06.2026)}

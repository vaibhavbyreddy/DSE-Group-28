This section investigates controllability and stability analyses on the HUGO aircraft. Newly developed software, Flow5, is used to demonstrate HUGO's capability of operation in various flight regimes and outputs several important values for the design, such as control surface actuator moments. The forces and moments on the control surfaces were calculated using the Flow5 software on a reference wing and tail planform used with HUGO. This software uses a potential flow solver, viscosity and wake models for preliminary estimation of aircraft characteristics. These include lift distribution, drag estimation, static stability and dynamic stability analysis. \footnote{\href{https://flow5.tech/docs/flow5_doc/flow5_doc.html}{Flow5 methods overview}(Accessed on 08.06.2026)} The aircraft was modeled and meshed in this software using the fuselage CAD model and a built-in wing generation tool, which was used for the main wing, tail and canard surfaces.

The NASA DAST drone featured ailerons with 23 \% of the chord length, 0.254 m in span (around 9\% of the half-wing span) and starting right at the wing root. This size is smaller than other reference aircraft, which is caused by the range of velocities the aircraft operated in. The horizontal empennage of the DAST drone did not feature an elevator. The exact size of the control surfaces in these aircraft is not available, manual measurements from projected views show that the chord fraction of the ailerons was close to 25 \%. Furthermore, the FLEXOP aircraft uses a V-tail empennage configuration with 2 elevators on each side. \subsection{Ailerons}
Based on the collected information, the roll characteristics were initially tested \todo{reference this} with ailerons having a 20 \% chord-to-length ratio, spanning 25 \% of the half-wing span and located at the wing center. These control surfaces were tested in an approximate approach speed regime, where $V_{appr} = 50 \ m \cdot s^{-1}$ at an angle of attack $\alpha=6.5 \degree$. The air used in the analysis had a zero-altitude ISA density, i.e. $\rho = 1.225 \ kg \cdot m^-3$. Each aileron has been set to 20 \degree deflection. At this flight velocity, the maximum calculated hinge moment on a single aileron is $0.0408 \ N\cdot m$, with both ailerons deflected in opposite directions. The resulting roll rate p is calculated using \autoref{eq:roll_rate} for steady-state roll rate \cite{nelson1998flight}.
\begin{equation}
    p = -2 \frac{V}{b} \frac{C_{l_{\delta_{a}}}}{C_{l_{p}}}\delta_a
    \label{eq:roll_rate}
\end{equation}
Here, $V = V_{appr}$, $b = 2.66m$ is the wing span, $C_{l_{\delta_{a}}}$ is the control derivative representing the change of $C_l$ with aileron deflection $\delta_a$ and $C_{l_{p}} = -0.76344$ is the stability derivative of the change of $C_l$ with the roll rate $p$ found using Flow5. The only unknown parameter is the change of the roll moment coefficient with aileron deflection $C_{l_{\delta_{a}}}$. This parameter was calculated at the approach speed $V_{appr} = 50 \ m \cdot s^{-1}$ from the change of $C_l$ between two aileron deflections $\delta_a = 0 \degree$ and $\delta_a = \pm0.1 \degree$. This resulted in $C_{l_{\delta_{a}}} = -0.19 \ rad^{-1}$. The roll rate $p$ at different aileron deflections $\delta_{a}$ are shown in \autoref{tab:delta_a_alpha}.

At the target experiment flight speed at $M = 0.75$, the required aileron deflection to reach the approach roll rate has been found to be $\delta_a = \pm3.92 \degree$. Flow5 outputs a moment $H_a = 0.179 \ N \cdot m$ for a single aileron at this speed regime. This speed lies in compressibility region, therefore accounting for this in the hinge moment is needed. Using the expression in \autoref{eq:hinge_moment} \cite{}, value obtained in Flow5 was then scaled using the Prandtl-Glauert compressibility correction in \autoref{eq:ch_compressibility}, analogous to the method Simpson used \cite{Simpson2016}.

\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:hinge_moment}
    C_h =\frac{H_a}{qS_ac_a} \approx 0.0345
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:ch_compressibility}
    C_{h_{c}} = \frac{C_h}{\sqrt{1-M^2}} = \frac{0.0345}{\sqrt{1-0.75^2}} \approx0.0522
\end{equation}
From this, the updated aileron hinge moment $H_{a_{c}} \approx 0.27\  N \cdot m$ was obtained. This value is important for the aileron actuator selection and overall aileron moment magnitude assessment.

The elevator effectiveness was estimated by perturbing the elevator deflection about the trim condition and computing the resulting change in pitching-moment coefficient. The elevator control derivative is approximated as

\begin{equation}
    C_{m_{\delta_e}} \approx
    \frac{C_m(\delta_e=+\Delta \delta_e)-C_m(\delta_e=0)}
    {\Delta \delta_e},
    \label{eq:cm_delta_e_numeric}
\end{equation}

where \(\Delta \delta_e\) is expressed in radians. The resulting value from Flow5 was

\begin{equation}
    C_{m_{\delta_e}} = {insert value}~\mathrm{rad^{-1}}.
\end{equation}

For each flight case, the required elevator deflection was determined by imposing longitudinal trim,

\begin{equation}
    C_m(\alpha,\delta_e,x_{cg}) = 0.
    \label{eq:longitudinal_trim_condition}
\end{equation}

The resulting trim deflections and hinge moments are listed in \autoref{tab:elevator_trim_results}. The most critical case for elevator sizing is expected to occur at the forward centre-of-gravity limit, where the elevator must generate the largest nose-up trim moment. For the high-speed condition, the elevator hinge moment was corrected using the same first-order Prandtl--Glauert approach as used for the ailerons. The elevator hinge-moment coefficient is defined as

\begin{equation}
    C_{h,e} =
    \frac{H_e}{q S_e c_e},
    \label{eq:elevator_hinge_coeff}
\end{equation}

where \(S_e\) and \(c_e\) are the elevator area and elevator chord, respectively. The compressibility-corrected coefficient is then

\begin{equation}
    C_{h,e,c} =
    \frac{C_{h,e}}{\sqrt{1-M^2}}.
    \label{eq:elevator_pg_correction}
\end{equation}

The corresponding corrected elevator hinge moment is

\begin{equation}
    H_{e,c} = C_{h,e,c} q S_e c_e.
    \label{eq:elevator_corrected_hinge_moment}
\end{equation}

The final elevator actuator should therefore be selected using the largest corrected hinge moment from \autoref{tab:elevator_trim_results}, multiplied by an appropriate safety factor. A preliminary sizing requirement can be written as

\begin{equation}
    H_{act,e} \geq SF \cdot \max \left( |H_{e,c}| \right),
    \label{eq:elevator_actuator_requirement}
\end{equation}

where \(SF\) is the actuator sizing safety factor. A value of \(SF=\todo{insert value, e.g. 1.5 or 2.0}\) is recommended for preliminary sizing.
\subsection{Dynamic characteristics of the aircraft}
Eigenmotions of HUGO were studied in order to assess the dynamic behavior of the aircraft within its flight envelope. Both longitudinal and lateral eigenmotions were investigated for both the canard and non-canard configuration using the Flow5 software. The aircraft was re-modeled based on the CAD model and included identical geometric, mass and inertial properties. \autoref{fig:flow5_overview} shows the aircraft properties used in this simulation. \autoref{fig:longitudinal_dynamic_modes} shows the modal responses in the longitudinal stability direction with the horizontal velocity disturbance $u$, vertical velocity disturbance $w$, pitch angle $\theta$ and pitch rate $q$. \autoref{fig:lateral_directional_dynamic_modes} shows characteristic modes for the lateral stability direction with lateral velocity disturbance $v$, roll rate $p$, yaw rate $r$ and bank angle $\varphi$. The modal response plots were not interpreted as physical aircraft attitude or rate amplitudes. In a linear modal analysis, each mode is represented by an eigenvector, whose magnitude is arbitrary. Multiplying the eigenvector by any scalar gives the same mode behavior. Flow5 does not allow for scaling the eigenvector magnitudes, therefore the unscaled eigenmode responses are shown as plotted by the software.

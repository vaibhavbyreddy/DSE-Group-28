As described by the project objectives, HUGO's mission is to serve as a flying lab for university researchers for whom testing with own full-sized aircraft or wind tunnels is unfeasible, as well as companies seeking to test their aerospace products. Therefore, a set of top-level requirements was defined by the Delft University of Technology (TUD) staff as part of general project guidelines \cite{Castro2026}. Additionally, external experts from the Netherlands Aerospace Centre (NLR) were consulted to establish a cumulative list of user requirements, which can be seen in \autoref{tab:user_requirements}. This list will be revisited in \autoref{sec:compliance_matrix_feasibility_analysis} and assessed using a compliance matrix to ensure that HUGO can fulfil all of its intended purposes.

The user requirements provide a sense of the aircraft's intended operation and the user's expectations regarding its final performance and design. As such, they cannot always be validated to ensure that the system can perform its functions successfully, i. e. they are not VALID (Verifiable, Achievable, Logical, Integral, Definite). For this reason, system requirements are defined to describe the product's performance in a way that can be validated. From those, subsystem requirements are derived to define the specific performance on a subsystem level. Comprehensive lists of system and subsystem requirements are showcased in \autoref{tab:system_requirements} and \autoref{tab:subsystem_requirements}, respectively.

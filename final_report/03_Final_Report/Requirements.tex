\chapter{Requirements Specification}
\label{ch:requirements}

The engineering design of HUGO has to be constrained by verifiable technical requirements to ensure that the final product is operational and fulfils all of its intended functions. In this chapter, the project objectives identified throughout the design process are revisited, and the process behind developing system and subsystem requirements is outlined. A description of the final project objectives is provided in \autoref{sec:project_objectives}, followed by the identification of critical system functions in \autoref{sec:identification_system_functions}. The findings from both sections are subsequently translated into a list of user, system, and subsystem requirements in \autoref{sec:user_requirements}.



\section{Project Objectives}
\label{sec:project_objectives}

The project objectives are formulated according to the SMART method: they are specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound. They serve as a roadmap for planning and execution, defining the tangible deliverables and outcomes required within a project’s timeline, budget, and scope. While these project objectives provide the high-level project ambitions, they are quantitatively defined and validated through the system and subsystem requirements outlined in \autoref{sec:user_requirements}.

\subsection{Financial Objectives}
The financial objectives focus on the economics of the project and the viability of this venture.

\begin{enumerate}

    \item Affordability: The total manufacturing cost per unit shall not exceed \texteuro{45000} \footref{foot:baseline}.
    
    \item Market capture: Companies should be able to test new components and achieve certification faster and for a lower cost compared to current testing methods.
    
    \item Academic research: The product must be accessible as a flying laboratory for universities with limited budgets.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Quality Objectives}
The quality objectives set the standard to which the design will be developed.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Longevity: The aircraft must have a minimum operational lifetime of 15 years.
    \item Serviceability and Inspection: Every subsystem and component of the aircraft must be easily accessible for inspection and maintenance.
    \item Reliability: Single-point electronic or mechanical failures must not result in mission failure, catastrophic loss, or ground hazards.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Performance Objectives}
The performance objectives focus on the capability to deliver results of the design.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Transonic testing: The aircraft must be able to sustain flight speeds between Mach 0.4 and Mach 0.75 for a minimum test time of 5 minutes.
    \item Endurance and range: The aircraft must be able to fly for a minimum of 30 minutes, with at least a 5-minute period of high-speed testing, whilst maintaining continuous telemetry over 165 km of range.
    \item Mass and payload capacity: The aircraft must not have a Maximum Take-Off Mass (MTOM) larger than 50 kg, and must accommodate at least 5 kg and 5 L of payload.
    \item Stability: The aircraft must be able to accommodate statically and dynamically stable, unstable, and neutrally stable flight conditions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Business Objectives}
The business objectives indicate the standard to which the organisation will perform its tasks.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Modular Platform: The aircraft should be a highly versatile platform capable of performing different tests with different configurations during the same day. 
    \item Logistics: The aircraft should fit within a standard, commercially available van. All additional maintenance components and ground station equipment must also fit within this van.
    \item Sustainability: The aircraft must fly with sustainable aviation fuel (SAF).
    \item Netherlands-centric: The Netherlands shall serve as the primary target market, with the aircraft taking off from Den Helder Airport.
\end{enumerate}


\subsection{Compliance Objectives}
The compliance objectives ensure adherence to regulations.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item EASA certification: The aircraft must be fully certifiable under EASA's SC Light-UAS framework.
    \item Autonomy: The aircraft should meet beyond visual line of sight (BVLOS) requirements, autonomously preventing catastrophic mission failure in case of communication loss.
\end{enumerate}


\section{Identification of System Functions}
\label{sec:identification_system_functions}
An understanding of the system's functions was obtained by constructing a Functional Flow Diagram (see: \autoref{sec:diagram}), from which the Functional Breakdown Structure was derived (see: \autoref{sec:diagram}). The intended performance of the final product was described in three levels, ensuring that all critical functions are identified and can later be turned into requirements.

Together with the general project guidelines \cite{Castro2026}, the identified functions were additionally used as a foundation for a more detailed task distribution definition developed by the Project Management (PM) department, described in more detail in \autoref{sec:pm_task_distribution}.

Therefore, a set of top-level requirements was defined by the Delft University of Technology (TUD) staff as part of general project guidelines \cite{Castro2026}. Additionally, external experts from the Netherlands Aerospace Centre (NLR) were consulted to establish a cumulative list of user requirements, which can be seen in \autoref{tab:user_requirements}. The user requirements provide a sense of the aircraft's intended operation and the user's expectations regarding its final performance and design. As such, they cannot always be validated to ensure that the system can perform its functions successfully, i. e., they are not VALID (Verifiable, Achievable, Logical, Integral, Definite). For this reason, the FFD and WBS were consulted to define the system requirements, which describe the product's performance in a way that can be validated. From those, subsystem requirements are derived to define the specific performance on a subsystem level. Comprehensive lists of system and subsystem requirements are showcased in \autoref{tab:system_requirements} and \autoref{tab:subsystem_requirements}, respectively.

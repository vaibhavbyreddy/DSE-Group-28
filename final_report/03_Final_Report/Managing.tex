The first stage of HUGO's design process defined the requirements and scope of the project, leading to the development of four distinct configurations. To narrow this solution space to a single, optimal design, a rigorous trade-off analysis was performed. This section summarises the characteristics of the four configurations, details the trade-off procedure, and introduces the final configuration that forms the basis of this report.

\section{Trade-Off Summary}
\label{sec:trade-off_summary}

Given the strict requirements and ambitions of the project, the design of HUGO rapidly converged towards a lightweight, fixed-wing drone with varying degrees of modularity. Therefore, four different configurations were developed to accommodate, depicted in \autoref{fig:hugo-configurations}, were developed to allow researchers to perform different sets of experiments, in different manners. 

HUG-CFG-301 (\autoref{fig:hug-cfg-301}) is a low-wing concept with static wing mounts and the ability to install a canard. HUG-CFG-302 (\autoref{fig:hug-cfg-302}) is instead a high-wing drone, with static wing mounts and no canard mounting port. HUG-CFG-303 (\autoref{fig:hug-cfg-303}) maintains the high-wing and static wing mounts, and further adds the ability to mount a canard. Lastly, HUG-CFG-304 (\autoref{fig:hug-cfg-304}) has a high-wing configuration with variable mounting locations for the main wing, as well as a static canard port.

\section{Engineering Design and Analysis}
\label{sec:engineering_design_and_analysis}

To fulfill its mission as a versatile flight test vehicle, HUGO is expected to house a range of wing planforms, later referred to as the Planform Envelope. This approach shall for a wide range of missions to be performed, examples of which can be found in \autoref{sec:mission_envelope}. The need to house multiple wing geometries has been a system design-driving characteristic since the midterm design phase. The goals of engineering analysis for the final work phase are to design HUGO's fuselage and its associated subsystems, ensuring they accommodate the specified range of planforms provided by client, as well as develop a standard planform to be used for external system tests that do not require a dedicated wing profile.

Several analyses described in the Midterm Report \footnote{Midterm Report} can be used for this design phase, i. e. the drag estimation, tail sizing, and fuel mass fraction computation. The corresponding matching diagram can also be re-implemented. Based on these findings, a structural analysis of both the wing planform and fuselage can be performed to obtain a complete estimate of aircraft weight. Further design steps include a preliminary sizing of the fuselage, along with a planform aileron sizing.

Considering the wide scope of possible array designs, featuring more than one possible airfoil, the approach to aerodynamic analysis differs from the one of the Midterm Report \footnote{Midterm Report}. Instead of defining an airfoil and conducting a lifting line analysis to obtain the properties of the three-dimensional wing directly, an envelope of possible airfoils is defined implicitly by ranges of values of lift slope, maximum lift and moment coefficients. The properties of the three-dimensional wing are deduced from those coefficients using semi-empirical formulae.

An additional challenge posed by the Planform Envelope is that it is a continuous design space spanning several parameters. Evaluating all combinations of those parameters at high resolution is not feasible. As such, a list of parameter combinations considered most constraining is justified in \todo{autoref}.

To allow for general-purpose testing of control systems, the standard planform of HUGO needs to be free of aeroelastic instability. To ensure that, an aeroelastic analysis of the planform is conducted  \todo{autoref}. The standard planform also serves as the benchmark for evaluating the performance of the aircraft in \autoref{ch:performance_analysis}. The influence of assumptions made during the design process on the ability of HUGO to meet its requirements and on the performance of the system needs to be assessed by means of a sensitivity study. This is addressed in \todo{autoref}.

\section{Boundaries of the Planform Envelope}
\label{sec:Planform envelope boundaries}

The Planform Envelope requires quantification - both for the user (to serve as specifications for wing design), and for further development. It is expected that a potential clients interested in planform research shall express interest in testing a wing of a particular geometry, not size, so long as the difference in Reynolds and Froude numbers can be accounted for. As such, the design specifications provided by the client are: the aspect ratio, taper, dihedral, sweep and thickness-to-chord profiles, which need not be constant along the wing. Additionally, the client is expected to specify wing cross sections along the span, provided the airfoil coefficients of those cross sections fit within the specified range. The wing area and structural thicknesses are deduced from the provided specifications.

\autoref{tab:planform_envelope} provides the range of allowed planform parameters. A planform is considered compatible with HUGO if it is described by any combination of the parameters within their ranges. To assess whether HUGO can accommodate those possible planforms, the most constraining values of each parameter are identified, and planforms represented by combinations of those critical values are analysed for compliance with requirements. Based on the number of identified critical value combinations, 96 planforms representing the critical envelope points can be defined. The critical values and parameter ranges were based on the following reasoning:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $AR$: the lower bound corresponds to low-$AR$ aerobatic aircraft characteristics \footnote{\label{foot:Aerobatic_AR}\href{https://web.archive.org/web/20060414172013/http://www.sukhoi.org/eng/planes/civil/su-26/characteristic/}{Sukhoi Su-26 Specifications}}, while the upper bound is as considered in previous reports \footnote{Baseline}\footnote{Midterm}. As increasing the $AR$ improves the aerodynamic efficiency while increasing wing weight, a single value of this parameter cannot be considered constraining. Therefore, four values from across the range are included as critical.
    \item $\lambda$: any taper profile that results in the wing not widening while moving inboard, but preventing it from becoming excessively thin, is considered acceptable. In the considered range, the most constraining case, when both aerodynamics and structural performance are negatively impacted, is when $\lambda$ is 1 \cite[p.401-405, p.438]{raymer1992aircraft, AndersonAerodynamics}.
    \item $\Lambda_{c/4}$: positive sweeps up to slightly above the Midterm Report value \footnote{Midterm Report}. The extremum values of sweep are taken as critical, as for maximum sweep the effective airspeed is reduced, and the aerodynamic centre is most aft, and for the minimum sweep the aerodynamic centre is most fore. The former is challenging for tail sizing for stability and main wing performance, while for the latter, empennage sizing for controllability is compromised. 
    \item $t/c$: The range encompasses thin and potentially unstable wings and high-thickness profiles similar to those at wing roots of large aircraft \footnote{\label{foot:reference_tc_values}\href{https://m-selig.ae.illinois.edu/ads/aircraft.html}{David Lednicer, The Incomplete Guide to Airfoil Usage}}. As for the critical points, parasitic drag increases with $t/c$ \cite[p.283]{raymer1992aircraft}, but the wing weight decreases significantly \cite[p.401-405]{raymer1992aircraft}. Thus, three values across the parameter range are selected for evaluation.
    \item $C_{l_\text{max}}$: To ensure sufficient low-speed performance, a minimum value of this parameter is established as 1, which is below the $C_{l_\text{max}}$ of most aifoils \footnote{\label{foot:NACA_4415}\href{http://airfoiltools.com/airfoil/details?airfoil=naca4415-il}{Airfoil Tools, NACA 4415}}\footnote{\label{foot:NACA_24112}\href{http://airfoiltools.com/airfoil/details?airfoil=naca24112-jf}{Airfoil Tools, NACA 24112}}.
    \item $C_{m_{ac}}$: The extrema values of this parameter are most constraining for sizing the empennage for controllability and, as such, taken as critical points. The minimum $C_{m_{ac}}$ accounts for high camber sections such as NACA 4415 with $C_{m_{ac}}$ of around -0.12 \footref{foot:NACA_4415}, while the upper bound accounts for reflexed airfoils such as NACA 24112  with $C_{m_{ac}}$ of up to 0.03 \footref{foot:NACA_24112}.
    \item $C_{l_0}$: The impact of this parameter is secondary - provided $C_{l_\alpha}$ and $C_{l_{\mathrm{max}}}$ are satisfactory, it only changes the angle of attack required for a given amount of lift. Hence, any non-negative vlue of the parameter is allowed, with zero taken as the critical value.
    \item $C_{l_\alpha}$: The lift slope for thin airfoil is typically close to $2\pi$ and this value is taken as critical.
    \item Empennage type: both replaceable tail cone and canard port are available at the aircraft, and they may be used simultaneously. As the moment arms of both the tail and the canard are fixed, cases when only the canard or only the tail are present are taken as constraining.
    \item $\Gamma$: The lower bound of dihedral at zero is set not to worsen the wing tip clearance, and the upper bound is where the lift reduction from dihedral becomes approximately 1\%. Due to its secondary influence, $\Gamma$ is excluded from the analysis, with a value of 0 used for landing gear clearance computations.
\end{itemize}

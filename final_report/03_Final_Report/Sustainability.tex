\chapter{Sustainability}
\label{ch:sustainability}
 This chapter looks into the environmental and social impact that HUGO has throughout its life. The economic aspect of any engineering project is vital for the feasibility of the system. This implies understanding the external economic factors and how they might impact the design. Just as important it is to look at how the system affects its surroundings. This is done by thoroughly describing the sustainability of the design. In this analysis, sustainability has been split into several sub-categories. In \autoref{sec:environmental_sustainability} the environmental sustainability which describes the system's impact on the environment, both the direct connected to the operation and less direct by the research it might enable. \autoref{sec:social_sustainability} describe the social sustainability. This is taking a more anthropological approach focusing on how it impacts people in the vicinity and society in general. Then in \autoref{sec:economic sustainability} the economic sustainability is introduced. This acts as an extension of the market analysis to ascertain that the system can operate without a net-loss. Finally, in \autoref{sec:LCA} the approach to the LCA is described. This assessment was performed to be able to quantify the impact of the system.

 
\section{Environmental Sustainability}
\label{sec:environmental_sustainability}

The main environmental benefit of this project is the research the platform will enable. One of the main focuses of the experiments conducted by HUGO is the study of high aspect ratio wings. In some modern airliners, almost 80 \% of the drag  during the climb-phase is lift induced drag and during cruise that number is around 40\% \cite{MA2024100983}. This lift-induced drag can be alleviated by increasing the aspect ratio of the wing \cite{MA2024100983}. Some methods have already been implemented to artificiality increase the aspect ratio with foldable wing-tips and winglets \footnote{\href{https://www.easa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/dfu/Consultation\%20-\%20SC\%20D-21-9\%20-\%20FINAL.PDF}{EASA Special Condition D-21-9, applicable to the Boeing 777-9} (accessed 17.06.2026)}. It has been estimated that the implementation of larger aspect ratio wings could reduce the fuel-burn of future aircraft with up to 10\% \cite{MA2024100983} . Increasing the aspect ratio dramatically changes the behaviour of the wing with one of the major areas affected being aeroelastic behaviour \cite{AFONSO201740}. As wings with a higher aspect ratio are more susceptible to flutter, the wing needs to be reinforced \footnote{\href{https://www.dlr.de/en/ae/latest/technical-articles/preliminary-aeroelastic-stability-assessment-of-high-aspect-ratio-wing-aircraft}{Flutter} (accessed on 12.06.2026)}.
The addition of structural reinforcement in the wing leads to more weight thereby potentially reducing the benefits of the decrease in drag.   
There are two major benefits in understanding aeroelastic behaviour: firstly, it enables the implementation and safe operation of a very high aspect ratio wing. Secondly, it enables the design of control systems which can alleviate loads thereby potentially leading to weight-reduction \cite{AN202048}.  

HUGO is designed with versatility in mind. By enabling the changing of wing-platforms it is possible to broaden the usability of the aircraft. There are currently several approaches to the future wing planforms, as well as several mounting points made on the fuselage for wing support struts, increasing the design freedom even further. This means that a small modification on an existing airframe can be made instead of the construction of an entirely new aircraft. This means much fewer resources have to be used for production, and the subsequent amount of waste material is reduced.   

Another benefit of the design versatility is its potential to reduce the development time of fuel-saving technologies. The same platform can be adapted for testing different concepts, allowing resources to be focused on technology development instead of creating a new aircraft.  The type of research enabled by HUGO will more accurately predict aeroelastic behaviour and other phenomena that are difficult to model in a wind-tunnel \cite{Stenfelt2015}. These factors can lead to a shorter development time and thereby lead to a faster utilisation of novel concepts. A fast implementation will lead to a larger overall reduction in aviation emissions.  

As stated by the user requirement, the aircraft must be entirely powered by SAF. Using SAF would reduce the net-emissions for the operation. Even with the relatively small scale of the aircraft, it was decided to try to minimise any impact on the surroundings. This was not only done in an effort to reduce the environmental impact of HUGO but also with the belief that the experience and knowledge gained would contribute to the overall implementation of SAF. 


\section{Social Sustainability}
\label{sec:social_sustainability}

One of the major incentives for the project was the possibility of the democratisation of high-level aerospace research. The phenomenon which can be investigated with this aircraft was traditionally only reserved for very large and expensive wind-tunnels or very complex purpose built prototypes.

As described in the certification (\autoref{sec:type_certificate}), logistics and manufacturing section (\autoref{sec:operations}), it takes a lot of effort to build and operate a prototype aircraft. Most of these resources do not go to the implementation of novel technologies but instead the application of existing components and methods. This causes a lot of time and resources to be spent on non-research activities. In the early literature review of the project, it became apparent that it have mostly been large organisations such as NASA which have been able to build and operate prototypes for just investigating one specific phenomenon. HUGO will enable small aerospace institutions to test their ideas and prototypes, thereby widening the scope of the influx of ideas for novel configurations and designs.  
 
%With its speed, relatively small size and low production cost HUGO can also be modified to be used as a weapon. This was something that under no circumstance should happen. Therefore early one it was decided that the ownership of every produced drone should be kept to management of HUGO. It would not be possible for anyone to buy the drone itself. In that way it is ensured that the drone would only be used for scientific purposes.  

Throughout the design phase, the potential noise generated by HUGO was discussed. It was decided that if the noise generated by the aircraft exceeds 90 dBn (see HUG-GEN-07) within a vicinity of 50 meters modifications would need to be implemented to mitigate volume. The 90 dB is dictated by the lower bounds set up in EU environmental legislation \cite{ICAO_Annex16_Vol1_2017}. Technically, HUGO is not legally required to follow this, but it was decided to do so to ensure a responsible operation of the aircraft. The noise of the aircraft was modelled and calculated for the mission profile in \autoref{sec:noise_characteristics}. 

Certain emissions generated by the jet turbine are unhealthy for humans. These would be emissions such as soot and carbon-monoxide \cite{masiol2014aircraft}. Part of the choice of the airport and most of the operation being over the ocean was driven by the intention to minimise the proximity to high population areas.

Aeroelastic effects have plagued jet aviation since its inception. Flutter is a dangerous phenomenon during flight and has been responsible for several crashes throughout history \cite{nasa2011modeling}. Therefore, it is relevant to understand the phenomena to ensure safe operation of passenger flights. The ability to accurately estimate the behaviour of wing-planforms will enable the design of safer aircraft.  

Another element concerning the social aspect is the integration of novel control systems such as gust alleviation. This does not only increases the design freedom of the aircraft but could also potentially dampen out turbulence, which often is a nuisance for passengers.

\section{Economic Sustainability}
\label{sec:economic sustainability}


This product remains uniquely positioned in the market. It has the potential to disrupt the traditional wind-tunnel testing market structure. At the time of this analysis, no other company can offer a comparable service as HUGO. This enables the freedom to set the pricing of the services.   

A detailed market analysis was made for the system which can be found (\autoref{ch:market_anaysis}). The overall conclusion was that the production and subsequent operation of HUGO would be able to generate a profit for the operators.  

\section{Life Cycle Assessment}
\label{sec:LCA} 

An LCA is the complete description of the inputs and outputs of a product from design until retirement. It can be used to assess the total impact of a system \footnote{\href{https://ecochain.com/blog/life-cycle-assessment-lca-guide/}{LCA} (accessed on 12.06.2026)}.    

An LCA consists of several sub-process which are interconnected. The life of the aircraft was split into three overall separate processes: the production of the aircraft, the operation of the and finally end-of-life. Each of those processes would consist of sub-processes. Every process takes one or several inputs and produces one or several outputs.

The open-source software, openLCA, was used for the construction of the LCA of HUGO.  Instead of starting from scratch for every component and material used in the LCA, the Swiss Federal Administration LCI Database \footnote{\href{https://esu-services.ch/bafu2025/}{BAFU:2025} (accessed on 12.06.2026)} was used. This holds detailed descriptions and a defined process for 11000 different components and processes \footnote{\href{https://nexus.openlca.org/database/BAFU}{BAFU} (accessed on 12.06.2026)}. Certain components and materials were unavailable in the BAFU:2025 database. When possible, a comparable component was selected, while in other cases, external information was used to define a new process.
In all of those cases the sources used for the estimate are listed in the description together with the sources used.  

For the production of the aircraft, the inputs would be all the subsystems: electrical, wings, fuselage, empennage, turbine, landing gear and pylons. These are then used as input for the operation of the aircraft. For the operation fuel, transportation and electricity were also used as inputs.   The end of life operation uses as inputs the scrapes after the UAV is dismantled.

Using the preliminary LCA it was possible to identify that the operations phase and more specifically the production and the combustion of the fuel are the largest contributors to resource usage. The first choice of fuel was a SAF produced using the Hydroprocessed Esters and Fatty Acids (HEFA) method, using soybeans imported from Brazil. After performing a preliminary LCA it became apparent that the emissions connected to the production of soybeans would be larger than the emissions captured by the soybean plants. This is due to the extensive deforestation in Brazil done to make room for soybean production \footnote{\href{https://www.sei.org/features/brazilian-soy-exports-and-deforestation/}{Brazil} (accessed on 12.06.2026)}. Therefore, it was decided to switch to rapeseed oil, which can be locally produced in Europe. The same method for its the refinement into SAF was utilised. 

Having constructed the LCA for the operation of the aircraft the final LCA will first be run for the final configuration when all elements and subsystems have been sized. Therefore, the final LCA is placed in the end of the report (\autoref{sec:finalLCA}). 

HUGOs' environmental requirements are closely regulated by the EU through general regulations and safety conditions that it has to comply with. To ensure that the UAV is allowed to perform its missions, it has to identify the risks associated with its operation and show that it complies with the certification procedures. These aspects are analysed in \autoref{ch:risk_assessment}.

The centre of gravity margin must be taken into account to meet the stability performance requirements. Specific location of the CG is driven by HUGO's external configuration, as well as the internal layout (i. e. electronics, propulsion system, payload). HUGO is expected to accommodate a wide variety of wing planforms, tail cones, and payload configurations, as well as operate in both stable and unstable regimes. As such, no dedicated CG range budget is defined. Rather, a CG analysis shall be performed for each configuration case to ensure that desired stability characteristics are achieved.

\subsection{Communication Link Budget}
Providing a reliable communication link at Mach 0.75 is a challenge for a drone of HUGOs' size since it cannot house complex radio systems. Since the mission profile states that the dorne must reach distances up to 110 km at MAch 0.75, a C2 \footnote{\href{https://eudroneport.com/blog/c2-links/}{C2 Communication Link} (accessed on 03.06.2026)} link protocol must be provided. This custom protocol is needed along with an efficient TX/RX combo because at 0.75 MAch, the radio waves will experience the Doppler effect. Using equation from \cite[p.477]{giancoli2020physics} calculating the Doppler effect, it was determined that the frequency can change up to 4 times the normal value.  

C2 is a custom-made protocol that allows for bidirectional communication at such speeds and for a smooth video and radio transmission. Due to the speed regime and distance at witch the test take place, the custom link will be programmed and modeld to fit the test cases. For HUGO, this protocol is paired with the electronic system, developed in \autoref{sub:electronics_system_design}. At the receiving end, there is a very powerful antenna still required, but this aspect is covered by the Den Helder, as mentioned in \autoref{ch:operation_and_logistics}, it is equipped with all the necessary equipment \footnote{\href{https://eaip.lvnl.nl/web/eaip/AIRAC\%20AMDT\%2005-2026_2026_05_14/eAIP/EH-AD\%202\%20EHKD\%201-en-GB.html}{Den Helder Info} (accessed 03.06.2026)}. The budget limitations for this system are brought by the electronic system, and due to the choice made in \autoref{sub:electronics_system_design}, HUGO is limited to VHF and UHF.

\subsection{Environmental Control}
\label{sub:environmental_control}
Environmental control is the response of HUGO to different external factors such as rain or sun radiation. An analysis was established were different flight conditions have been considered, and it was determined that the critical case for HUGO come from the presence of ice on the wings due to the low temperatures. 

As supercooled water hits the fuselage, it instantly turned into ice, increasing the weight \cite{andreev2014icing} and damaging the aerodynamic profile. Since the wings fitted on HUGO are very thin, optimised for high cruise speed, any perturbation of the aerodynamic profile may result in a catastrophic event. 

There are two primary way to combat this phenomenon, either by using a passive system (coating or phase changing materials) or b an active system (heating elements). Since the wings are subjected to regular replacement and modifications, it is more efficient to use a passive system for HUGO.

\subsection{Sensor Interaction}
\label{sub:sensor_interaction}
To be able to measure and record the results from the experiments it is necessary to use sensors. Since an aspect of HUGO is to possibility of  customizations for a specific customer needs there is left space in the design for additional sensors.     
Using the mission envelope  (autoref ops \& logistics) it is possible identify the following phenomenon which needs to be measured: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Deflection: This is how much the wing is moved from the original axis. There are several approaches for the measurement of wing deflection. A way to measure the deflection is to record it from a fixed point on the aircraft. A stereo camera setup can be used for this \cite{LIU2021107161}. A pattern with a high contrast (typically black and white)  would be placed on the wing. By having a reference image of the undistorted pattern it is possible to measure the change in the wing \cite{Veerman2008}. It is also possible to use LIDAR where small dots of reflective material would be placed on the wing.  Using the fixed points and looking at the subsequent change it is possible determine how the wing deforms \cite{opt5010011}.  A different approach is the use of special fiber-optic cables which can measure the strain in the axial direction of the cable. By afterwards overlying the points in a FE-model of the wing it is possible to find the deflection of the wing \cite{Pak2015}. The current systems architecture is made so it can accommodate all three different measurement techniques. This gives a potential costumer a larger freedom in choice regarding sensors.     
    \item Strain: A strain gage is a classical approach for measuring strain. It uses the changed resistance in a wire when mechanically deformed \footnote{\href{https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/strain-gage}{Strain gage}(02.06.2026)}. A typically configuration is a strain rosetta which consits three strain gages oriented 0,45,90 degrees. This makes it possible to measure the strain in all directions in the surface  \footnote{\href{https://intertechnology.com/Vishay/pdfs/TechNotes_TechTips/TN-515.pdf}{Strain gage}(02.06.2026)}.  
    
    \item Loads: In this instance the measurement of the loads acting on and through the wing is the area of interest. For an aircraft this would be the resultant force (most often split into lift and drag) generated by the wing.  
    
    \item Vibrations:
    \item Wind-gusts: LIDAR can be used to detect wind ahead of an aircraft. The sensors sends out laser-light and then measuring the Doppler-shift in the back-scattered light it is possible to estimate a magnitude and direction of the wind\cite{YEW2025101127}.  
\end{itemize}

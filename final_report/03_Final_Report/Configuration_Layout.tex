The centre of gravity margin must be taken into account to meet the stability performance requirements. Specific location of the CG is driven by HUGO's external configuration, as well as the internal layout (i. e. electronics, propulsion system, payload). HUGO is expected to accommodate a wide variety of wing planforms, tail cones, and payload configurations, as well as operate in both stable and unstable regimes. As such, no dedicated CG range budget is defined. Rather, a CG analysis shall be performed for each configuration case to ensure that desired stability characteristics are achieved.

The determination of the CG for a given configuration is done by means of segmentation. In this approach, the CG of the fuselage is isolated from the rest of the components, and can be used as a first estimate for wing sizing. The final position of the CG can be adjusted during design iterations by distributing the electronics and propulsion system components inside the aircraft. For test cases focused on stability and requiring an in-flight shift in CG (such as TBD TBD \autoref{}), its position can be adjusted by varying the fuel distribution between two bladders. \subsection{Communication Link Budget}
\label{sub:communication_link_budget}
Based on HUGO's mission profile, specifically the maximum flight speed and maximum distance from the aerodrome, a C2 communication link must be specified as part of the Type Design \cite{SC Light-UAS}. This custom protocol is required along with an efficient transmitter/receiver combination due to the Doppler shift, which becomes significant at the airspeed of 0.75 Mach. the radio waves will experience the Doppler effect. Using the Giancoli's formula for the Doppler shift \cite[p.477]{giancoli2020physics}, is can be estimated that the shifted frequency may attain values as high as four times its initial transmission counterpart (if HUGO performs the high-speed test flight in the direction of the observer), or as low as 57\% (if flying away from the observer).

C2 is a custom-made protocol that allows for bidirectional communication at such speeds and for a smooth video and radio transmission \footnote{\href{https://eudroneport.com/blog/c2-links/}{C2 Communication Link} (accessed on 03.06.2026)}. It shall be designed to fit the considered test cases. For HUGO, the C2 is paired with the electronic system, the design of which will be discussed in \autoref{sec:internal_configuration}. Appropriate radionavigation and landing equipment for the receiving end of C2 communication is available at Den Helder aerodrome \footnote{\href{https://eaip.lvnl.nl/web/eaip/AIRAC\%20AMDT\%2005-2026_2026_05_14/eAIP/EH-AD\%202\%20EHKD\%201-en-GB.html}{EHKD AD 2.19 Radio navigation and landing aids} (accessed 03.06.2026)}. The specific communication link budget is defined by the electronics system design and will be discussed later.

\subsection{Environmental Control}
\label{sub:environmental_control}
Environmental control is the response of HUGO to different external factors such as rain or UV-radiation. An analysis was established were different flight conditions have been considered, and it was determined that the critical case for HUGO come from the presence of ice on the wings due to the low temperatures. 

As supercooled water hits the fuselage, it instantly turns into ice, increasing the weight \cite{andreev2014icing} and damaging the aerodynamic profile. Since the wings fitted on HUGO are very thin, optimised for high cruise speed, any perturbation of the aerodynamic profile may result in a catastrophic event. 

There are two primary way to combat this phenomenon, either by using a passive system (coating or phase changing materials) or b an active system (heating elements). Since the wings are subjected to regular replacement and modifications, it is more efficient to use a passive system for HUGO. 

A coating that could help HUGO delay the formation of ice on the wings involves coating them with a poly-siloxane elastomer coating that sticks to resin \cite{facco2025passive}. It delays the formation of ice on the wings as well as offering a very low ice adhesion, effectively protecting the wing from ice. Despite its weak mechanical properties, HUGO is not meant to fly for many missions with the same set of wings, as such it does not require withstanding a lot of cycles.

To be able to measure and record the results from the experiments it is necessary to use sensors. Since an aspect of HUGO is to possibility of  customizations for a specific customer needs there is left space in the design for additional sensors.     
Using the mission envelope  (autoref ops \& logistics) it is possible identify the following phenomenon which needs to be measured: 
\begin{itemize}
    \item Deflection: This is how much the wing is moved from the original axis. There are several approaches for the measurement of wing deflection. A way to measure the deflection is to record it from a fixed point on the aircraft.

Using the fixed points and looking at the subsequent change it is possible determine how the wing deforms \cite{opt5010011} \cite{LopezHerreros2024}.  A different approach is the use of special fiber-optic cables which can measure the strain in the axial direction of the cable.     \item Strain: A strain gage is a classical approach for measuring strain. It uses the changed resistance in a wire when mechanically deformed \footnote{\href{https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/earth-and-planetary-sciences/strain-gage}{Strain gage}(02.06.2026)}. A typically configuration is a strain rosetta which consits three strain gages oriented 0,45,90 degrees. This makes it possible to measure the strain in all directions in the surface  \footnote{\href{https://intertechnology.com/Vishay/pdfs/TechNotes_TechTips/TN-515.pdf}{Strain gage}(02.06.2026)}.  
    
    \item Loads: In this instance the measurement of the loads acting on and through the wing is the area of interest. For an aircraft this would be the resultant force (split into lift and drag) generated by the wing. Load-cells are sensors which can measure a load in a specific direction. Certain load cells can also measure the moment-generated in all three axial directions. These are know as six-axis load cells.       
    
    \item Wind-gusts: LIDAR can be used to detect wind ahead of an aircraft\cite{Schroeder2020}. The sensors sends out laser-light and then measuring the Doppler-shift in the back-scattered light it is possible to estimate a magnitude and direction of the wind \cite{YEW2025101127}. 
\end{itemize}

It is also important to evaluate the precision and accuracy of each sensor. Precision refers to how much repeated measurements deviate for a specific sensor. The accuracy specifies how much the measured value deviates from the true value. The combination of these will make it possible to access the quality of the measured data.
